## 1. Purpose, Methodology, and Statutory Scope

This report presents the Preliminary Regulatory Scan under RFP 05 (`CPC-26-0014` / `ER-0004b-25`), executing the statutory and supervisory evaluation defined in Phase 3 and **Appendix C** of the Milestone 1 Research Design Report. It evaluates the statutory, licensing, data-protection, payment, and identity treatment of all eight candidate Cardano insurance use-case hypotheses across five Nigerian regulatory authorities:

1. **National Insurance Commission (NAICOM)**: Primary prudential and market-conduct supervisor for all insurance, reinsurance, microinsurance, Takaful, web-aggregation, and insurtech activities in Nigeria.
2. **Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Nigeria)**: Primary capital-market and virtual/digital-asset regulator overseeing distributed ledger technology (DLT) tokens, virtual asset service providers (VASPs), and fiat-referenced stablecoin issuance.
3. **Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN)**: Monetary authority and payments-system supervisor governing commercial bank settlement accounts for VASPs, Payment Service Banks (PSBs), mobile money operators (MMOs), NIBSS Instant Payments (NIP), and foreign-exchange rails.
4. **National Identity Management Commission (NIMC)**: Statutory custodian of the National Identity Database and the National Identification Number (NIN), governing identity verification access for regulated financial institutions.
5. **Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)**: Independent data-privacy regulator enforcing personal data processing principles, Data Privacy Impact Assessments (DPIAs), automated decision-making safeguards, erasure rights, and cross-border data transfer controls.

### 1.1 Classification Taxonomy (Per Milestone 1 Appendix C)

Each candidate use case is classified under one or more of the four regulatory postures established in Milestone 1 Appendix C:

- **Hard Prohibition:** A primary statute or binding regulatory instrument expressly forbids the core activity regardless of licensing structure. Under Milestone 1 Section 3.4, only a verified primary-statute Hard Prohibition eliminates a candidate use case at Gate A.
- **Conditional Permission:** The activity is legally permissible provided the operator holds a specified licence (or partners with a licensed institution under a regulator-approved Service Level Agreement) and complies with explicit statutory conditions or prior-approval gates.
- **Regulatory Silence:** Extant statutes and guidelines neither expressly authorise nor expressly prohibit the specific technical mechanism, leaving treatment subject to general contract, prudential, or data-protection principles.
- **Sandbox Pathway:** A formal regulatory incubation or sandbox regime exists through which the mechanism may be tested under supervisory waivers or observation prior to full commercial authorisation.

### 4.1 Scan 1: Use Case 1: Parametric Agricultural and Climate Micro-Insurance

### 4.3 Scan 3: Use Case 3: Shared Fraud-Resistant Claims and Policy Verification Registry

| Dimension | Verified Regulatory Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Relevant authorities** | **NAICOM** (under NIIRA 2025 s. 207 and Insurtech Guidelines 2025); **NDPC** (under NDPA 2023 ss. 24, 25, 28, 34, 41–43, 44); **Nigerian Insurers Association (NIA)** (industry self-regulatory body operating the NIID motor database). **SEC Nigeria** and **CBN** are **not triggered** (no financial asset or payment rail is involved). |
| **Classification** | **Conditional Permission** (Lowest regulatory friction among all eight candidate use cases). |
| **Key rules** | • **NIIRA 2025 s. 207(1)–(3) (Duty of Insurers to Report Fraudulent Conduct):** Mandates that where an insurer reasonably believes fraudulent insurance conduct is being or has been committed, it shall furnish information to NAICOM, and expressly permits voluntary disclosure to law enforcement agencies.<br/>• **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines 2025 §18.3(iii)(f)–(g):** Requires prior NAICOM approval before an insurer/insurtech engages a third-party technology vendor or alters server locations.<br/>• **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §9.1(ii) & §9.2(viii):** Mandates compliance with the **NDPA 2023**, the **Cybercrimes Act 2015**, and an **annual independent technology audit**. |
| **Licence/approval need** | 1. Can be operated either as a **B2B technical vendor / industry consortium utility** approved by NAICOM under **§18.3(iii)(f)** (in partnership with the NIA or participating insurers) or under a **Partnering Insurtech Licence** (**§4.1(e)** claims management support; ₦10m capital + ₦100m PI).<br/>2. Registration as a **Data Processor / Data Controller of Major Importance (DCPMI)** with the NDPC under **NDPA 2023 s. 44** and *NDPC GAID 2025*. |
| **Data & privacy** | **Critical Design Rule:** Placing a "fraud watchlist" containing plain-text names, NINs, BVNs, or medical/claim details onto an immutable public blockchain would violate **NDPA 2023 s. 24** (data minimisation/accuracy), **s. 30** (sensitive data), **s. 34(1)(d)** (right to erasure/rectification if a fraud flag is contested or overturned), and **ss. 41–43** (cross-border transfer to global validator nodes). **Compliant Architecture:** Only **salted, keyed cryptographic hashes (HMAC-SHA256 / Blake2b-256) or Midnight zero-knowledge commitments** of policy/claim attributes (e.g., hashed `VIN + LossDate + ClaimType`) are anchored on Cardano; raw evidentiary records remain in an off-chain, access-controlled domestic database hosted in Nigeria where records can be rectified or erased after the **10-year retention period (§17.2(ii))**. ## 5. Summary of Architectural Red Lines and Compliant Design Patterns

To guide Milestone 3 stakeholder interviews and subsequent technical architecture in Milestone 4, Table 5.1 distils the non-negotiable statutory "Red Lines" discovered during the Milestone 2 regulatory scan and pairs each with its legally compliant Cardano design pattern.

### Table 5.1: Statutory Red Lines vs Compliant Cardano Architectural Patterns

| Statutory Red Line (Source Provision) | Non-Compliant Blockchain Pattern (Prohibited) | Compliant Cardano Architectural Pattern (Permissible) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. No Crypto Premiums or Claims Without Prior NAICOM Approval**<br/>(*NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines 2025* **§12.1(vi)**) | Requiring Nigerian retail policyholders or smallholders to hold ADA/stablecoins in self-custody wallets to pay premiums or receive claims directly on-chain without NAICOM approval. | **Fiat-at-the-Edge / Ledger-at-the-Core:** Collect premiums and disburse claims in fiat NGN via NIBSS NIP / PSB / USSD; use Cardano (with fee-sponsored relayers) for index calculation, hash registries, SLA audit trails, and treaty accounting, or apply for §5.0(vi) Sandbox / §12.1(vi) approval for cross-border/diaspora flows. |
| **2. "No Premium, No Cover" & Instant Insurer Account Credit**<br/>(*NIIRA 2025* **s. 60(1)**; *Insurtech Guidelines* **§14.4**) | Locking retail policyholders' premiums inside an autonomous, unlicensed on-chain smart-contract liquidity pool (`DeFi risk pool`) before remitting to a licensed underwriter. | **Direct Insurer Custody + On-Chain State Mirror:** Fiat premiums credit the licensed underwriter's or Takaful *Tabarru'* bank account immediately via NIBSS/PSB split-settlement; the Cardano smart contract records the cryptographic receipt, pool accounting state, and payout trigger. |

| **4. Data Subject Right to Erasure & Sensitive PII Protection**<br/>(*NDPA 2023* **s. 24, s. 30, s. 34(1)(d), ss. 41–43**; *Insurtech Guidelines* **§9.1(ii)**) | Writing plain-text names, NINs, BVNs, farm GPS coordinates of named individuals, medical diagnoses, or fraud accusations onto the immutable public Cardano ledger. | **Off-Chain PII + On-Chain Salted Hashes / ZK Credentials:** Store all raw PII in encrypted, NDPA-compliant domestic databases (retained for 10 years per **§17.2(ii)** and erasable thereafter); anchor only salted `Blake2b-256` hashes, Merkle roots, **Hyperledger Identus** DIDs/revocation registries, or **Midnight** ZK proofs on Cardano. |

Mandatory Licensing for Web/Electronic Insurance & Insurtech**<br/>(*NIIRA 2025* **s. 200, s. 201**; *Insurtech Guidelines* **§§ 1.2, 14.1, 18.3(iii)(f)**) | Deploying an unlicensed consumer-facing dApp that sells insurance cover directly to Nigerian residents without a NAICOM licence. | **Partnering Insurtech Vehicle (§14.1):** Incorporate a Nigerian entity licensed as a **Partnering Insurtech** (₦10m capital + ₦100m Professional Indemnity) or approved B2B technology vendor (**§18.3(iii)(f)**) operating under a formal SLA (**§8.0**) with a NAICOM-licensed underwriter. |
