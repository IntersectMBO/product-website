## Summary

Milestone 2 executes the desk research and market framing (Phase 1/2), stakeholder ecosystem mapping (Phase 2), preliminary regulatory and infrastructure readiness assessment (Phase 3), and Gate A pre-fieldwork provisional screen established in the Milestone 1 Research Design Report. Before primary stakeholder interviews begin in Milestone 3, this milestone establishes the empirical, statutory, technical, and comparative baseline against which the eight candidate blockchain-enabled insurance use-case hypotheses are evaluated.

During this milestone, the research team:

- **Produced the Desk Research Report (Market Framing Document):** Established the empirical baseline of the Nigerian insurance market using National Insurance Commission (NAICOM), Nigerian Insurers Association (NIA), National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), and EFInA Access to Financial Services (A2F) datasets; mapped 25 peer-reviewed, statutory, and institutional sources across six analytical themes; evaluated all eight candidate use cases against non-blockchain alternatives and live Cardano mainnet protocol parameters; and executed a four-country Comparative African Screen across Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, and South Africa.
- **Completed the Preliminary Regulatory Scan:** Audited all eight candidate use cases across five Nigerian statutory authorities—NAICOM, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC Nigeria), the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN), the National Identity Management Commission (NIMC), and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (NDPC)—incorporating the *Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act (NIIRA) 2025*, the *NAICOM Guidelines for Insurtech Operations in Nigeria (August 2025)*, the *Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025*, the *Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023*, the *NIMC Act (2007/2026)*, and the *CBN VASP Account Guidelines (December 2023)*.
- **Constructed the Preliminary Stakeholder Ecosystem Map:** Catalogued **63 named institutions** across four stakeholder archetypes—Regulators, Standards-Setters, and Government Entities (Archetype A: 7 entities), Insurance Underwriters, Reinsurers, Microinsurers, and Takaful Operators (Archetype B: 22 entities), Insurtechs, Intermediaries, and Professional Bodies (Archetype C: 16 entities), and Extended Ecosystem, Payment, Identity, Agricultural, Beneficiary, and Cardano Technical Actors (Archetype D: 18 entities)—recording mandate, use-case alignment, locked Milestone 1 §4.4 access status, §6.4 sponsor-affiliation flags, §4.2 sampling quota crosswalk, and §4.6 five-wave recruitment sequencing.
- **Executed Gate A (Pre-Fieldwork Provisional Screen) and the Appendix D Infrastructure Prerequisite Matrix:** Scored all eight candidate use cases using desk evidence across the locked nine-criterion (`C1`–`C9`), four-level ordinal scale (`Strong [S]`, `Conditional [C]`, `Weak [W]`, `Disqualifying [D]`) from Milestone 1 Chapter 5, applying the three protective rules of Milestone 1 Section 3.4 to re-order interview emphasis for Milestone 3 without prematurely eliminating any hypothesis ahead of primary fieldwork, and mapped all infrastructure dependencies and Cross-RFP handoffs (`RFP 01`, `03`, `06`, `07`, `08`) under Milestone 1 Appendix D.
- **Prepared the Regional Screening Review Briefing Pack:** Consolidated the empirical findings, statutory red lines, comparative African benchmarks, and Gate A provisional priority tiers for review by the Cardano Product Committee (CPC).

**AI Disclosure (per Contract Clause 4.2):** AI-assisted tools were used for document structuring, formatting, and table layout during the preparation of milestone documentation. All statutory verification, empirical analysis, scoring judgements, and research conclusions were conducted and validated by the Principal Investigator and the research team, who remain solely responsible for accuracy.

## Challenges and Learnings

1. **Rapid statutory transition in Nigeria (2025–2026):** Between March 2025 and August 2026, Nigeria enacted the *Investments and Securities Act 2025*, enacted the *Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act (NIIRA) 2025*, brought the *NAICOM Guidelines for Insurtech Operations in Nigeria* into force (1 August 2025), enacted the *NIMC Act 2026* (June 2026), and completed a 12-month statutory insurance recapitalisation exercise (2 August 2026). Secondary commentary frequently cited repealed provisions of the *Insurance Act 2003* (such as Section 50(1) on premium prepayment, now Section 60(1) of NIIRA 2025) or imprecise composite capital figures. The research team resolved this by retrieving and auditing the primary gazetted and regulator-issued PDF texts directly.
2. **Explicit NAICOM rules on crypto-denominated insurance flows and automated claims rejections:** Direct inspection of Section 12.1 of the *NAICOM Guidelines for Insurtech Operations in Nigeria (2025)* revealed two provisions of immediate consequence for blockchain architecture: Section 12.1(vi) prohibits the acceptance of premiums or settlement of claims in cryptocurrency without prior NAICOM approval, and Section 12.1(iv) prohibits complete reliance on automated systems to decline claims without human intervention. These findings shift several candidate designs from "Regulatory Silence" to "Conditional Permission" and require fiat-abstracted or regulator-approved settlement structures.
3. **Cardano stablecoin and oracle infrastructure gaps in the Nigerian context:** While Cardano mainnet provides deterministic transaction fees and native token architecture, desk verification confirmed that Nigeria's regulated Naira stablecoin (`cNGN`) is deployed across eight non-Cardano chains and is not natively available on Cardano (`RFP 03` handoff), and that Cardano's native oracle providers (Charli3 and Orcfax) currently supply financial price feeds rather than calibrated Nigerian agro-meteorological feeds (`RFP 07` handoff). These empirical constraints directly informed the Gate A provisional ranking, the Appendix D Infrastructure Prerequisite Matrix, and the technical interview questions prepared for Milestone 3.
