## 1. Purpose and Governance Rules of Gate A

This document executes **Gate A (The Pre-Fieldwork Provisional Screen)** and the Phase 3 **Infrastructure Prerequisite Assessment** as defined in **Section 3.4**, **Chapter 5 (`Sections 5.1–5.6`)**, **Section 9.6**, and **Appendix D** of the Milestone 1 Research Design Report. Gate A sits at the boundary between desk research, regulatory scanning, and ecosystem mapping (Milestone 2) and primary stakeholder fieldwork (Milestone 3).

### 1.1 Strict Enforcement of Milestone 1 Section 3.4 Protective Rules and Section 6.3 Design Lock

In accordance with the **Design Lock Rule** of Milestone 1 Section 6.3, Gate A applies the exact nine criteria (`C1`–`C9`), four-level ordinal scale (`Strong`, `Conditional`, `Weak`, `Disqualifying`), and decision rules locked at project inception. Three mandatory protective rules from **Milestone 1 Section 3.4** govern this provisional screen:

1. **Rule 1: Explicit Desk-Evidence Labelling:** Every criterion score and provisional tier in this document is explicitly labelled **`[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`**. Where a criterion (such as Criterion 2: Stakeholder Demand) requires primary empirical fieldwork to confirm institutional or beneficiary willingness beyond desk indicators, the score is explicitly annotated to distinguish **insufficient desk evidence awaiting Milestone 3 fieldwork** from **affirmative desk evidence of a barrier**.
2. **Rule 2: Re-Ordering of Interview Emphasis Without Premature Elimination:** *"Gate A may re-order interview emphasis but may not eliminate a candidate. All eight remain in the interview guides. The only exception is a Disqualifying finding grounded in primary legal text (for example, a statute that expressly prohibits the function), documented and open to challenge in Phase 5."* Because the Preliminary Regulatory Scan ([Preliminary Regulatory Scan](./regulatory-preliminary-scan.md)) confirms that all eight candidate use cases possess legally permissible pathways under the *Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act (NIIRA) 2025*, the *NAICOM Guidelines for Insurtech Operations in Nigeria (2025)*, the *Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023*, and the *Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025* when structured within the statutory red lines identified in the [Preliminary Regulatory Scan](./regulatory-preliminary-scan.md), **zero candidate use cases receive a `Disqualifying (D)` score at Gate A, and all eight hypotheses (`UC1`–`UC8`) advance to Milestone 3 primary validation**.
3. **Rule 3: Clean-Slate Archival Prior to Phase 6:** *"Gate A scores are archived and never carried into the Phase 6 scorecard."* Once Milestone 3 interview allocation is set, Gate A desk scores are archived so that the final Phase 6 (Milestone 4) viability screen is scored from a clean slate grounded in triangulated primary and secondary evidence.

### Four-Level Ordinal Scoring Scale (Milestone 1 Section 5.3)

- **`Strong (S)`:** Clear desk evidence supporting viability; no significant structural barriers identified.
- **`Conditional (C)`:** Desk evidence supports viability subject to identifiable regulatory, architectural, or commercial conditions to be verified in fieldwork.
- **`Weak (W)`:** Desk evidence is either insufficient prior to primary fieldwork (`W* [Desk Evidence Insufficient: To Be Tested in M3]`) or identifies significant operational/regulatory/infrastructure barriers (`W [Significant Desk Barrier Identified]`).
- **`Disqualifying (D)`:** A structural barrier that cannot be resolved within 12 to 24 months (at Gate A, restricted strictly to a primary-statute Hard Prohibition per Milestone 1 Section 3.4 Rule 2).

## 4. Analytical Justification of Gate A Provisional Scores (`UC1`–`UC8`)

### 4.1 Tier 1: Primary Fieldwork Emphasis (`UC3`, `UC4`, `UC5`)

1. **Rank 1: UC3: Fraud-Resistant Shared Claims Registry (`7S · 2C · 0W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC3 leads at Gate A:** UC3 achieves the highest alignment across statutory mandate, blockchain necessity, infrastructure readiness, and regulatory permissibility. **NIIRA 2025 Section 207(1)** imposes a direct statutory duty on insurers to report fraudulent insurance conduct (`C1 = S`), while competing underwriters resist pooling raw policyholder databases with a commercial rival, satisfying **Wüst & Gervais's (2018)** multi-writer, no-single-trusted-commercial-operator test when implemented via salted `Blake2b-256` claim/VIN/invoice hashes on Cardano or zero-knowledge proofs on Midnight (`C3 = S`). Because no cryptocurrency or payment token is used, **SEC Nigeria and CBN digital-asset restrictions are completely untriggered**, and keeping raw PII off-chain in domestic storage satisfies **NDPA 2023 s. 34(1)(d)** (`C4 = S`, `C5 = S`). `C2` and `C7` are scored `C` because Milestone 3 interviews must test whether underwriters prefer an NIA-governed extension of the NIID motor database or an independent NAICOM-approved Partnering Insurtech utility.
2. **Rank 2: UC4: Reinsurance Treaty Reconciliation (`5S · 4C · 0W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC4 ranks second:** **NIIRA 2025 Section 204(6)** provides an explicit statutory opening by removing the in-person engagement requirement for approved foreign reinsurance placements under the annual reinsurance programme submitted to NAICOM, and **Section 60(5)** exempts reinsurance contracts from rigid retail prepayment rules (`C4 = S`). Multi-party state synchronisation across cedants, reinsurance brokers, and treaty reinsurers passes the blockchain necessity test, and ~0.30 ADA Plutus script execution fees are negligible against wholesale treaty balances (`C1 = S`, `C3 = S`). `C2`, `C5`, `C7`, and `C8` are scored `C` to reflect the **B3i consortium insolvency lesson (L06)**: Milestone 3 interviews with Leadway, NSIA, Continental Re, Africa Re, and NCRIB brokers must verify whether a lightweight CSV/ACORD-to-Cardano hash-reconciliation layer can overcome ERP switching costs.
3. **Rank 3: UC5: Claims Automation / Smart Adjudication (`3S · 6C · 0W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC5 ranks third:** Slow and disputed claims settlement is the primary driver of Nigeria's insurance trust deficit (THISDAYLIVE, 2026; EFInA, 2023), and **NIIRA 2025 Section 210(3) and (7)** now subjects the 43 post-recapitalisation insurers to a **60-day settlement deadline**, a ₦500,000 penalty plus **monthly compound interest at the prevailing bank rate**, and direct deduction from their CBN statutory deposit (`C1 = S`, `C8 = S`). However, `C3`, `C4`, and `C5` are scored `C` because: (a) **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §12.1(iv)** and **NDPA 2023 s. 37** strictly prohibit unsupervised automated claim rejections without human intervention (requiring an asymmetrical smart-contract design that auto-settles approved claims and logs 60-day SLA compliance while escalating all potential denials to a human adjuster); and (b) Cardano must demonstrate clear incremental audit value over established Web2 claims-automation platforms such as **Curacel**.

### 4.2 Tier 2: High-Impact Retail, Agricultural, Takaful, and Identity Emphasis (`UC1`, `UC2`, `UC7`)

4. **Rank 4: UC1: Parametric Agricultural Insurance (`2S · 6C · 1W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC1 leads Tier 2:** Smallholder climate vulnerability across >35 million farming households is acute (`C1 = S`), and **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §4.1(g)** explicitly authorises Partnering Insurtechs to conduct *"crop cutting, data collection and yield calculation"* alongside the **§5.0(vi) Regulatory Sandbox** (`C4 = C`). However, Criterion 5 (`Infrastructure readiness`) is scored **`W`** at desk stage because **neither Charli3 nor Orcfax currently provides calibrated Nigerian agro-meteorological feeds (NiMet ground stations or CHIRPS/NDVI satellite indices)** on Cardano mainnet, requiring a bespoke oracle adapter. Additionally, **Clarke (2016)** demonstrates that spatial basis risk suppresses rational index demand unless paired with multi-scale yield verification and input-credit bundling (`C2 = C`, `C3 = C`), and **NAICOM §12.1(vi)** requires payouts to settle in fiat Naira over NIBSS/PSB rails (`C8 = C`).
5. **Rank 5: UC2: Microinsurance Premium Collection and Pooling, Including Takaful (`2S · 7C · 0W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC2 ranks fifth:** Retail exclusion stands at 97% of Nigerian adults (EFInA, 2023) and demand for transparent Shariah-compliant *Tabarru'* risk-pool accounting is documented across Northern and national Takaful operators (`C1 = S`, `C6 = S`). However, desk verification established two critical boundaries: (a) **NIIRA 2025 Section 60(1)** ("No Premium, No Cover") and **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §14.4** (requiring instant credit of premiums to the licensed insurer's bank account) strictly bar unlicensed autonomous DeFi risk pools (`C4 = C`); and (b) Cardano's `4,310 lovelace/byte` min-UTxO deposit and ~0.17 ADA base fee make unbatched per-transaction L1 micro-premiums uneconomic against NIBSS/PSB fiat rails (`C3 = C`, `C5 = C`). UC2 is therefore viable specifically as a **daily-batched cryptographic audit rail mirroring a bank-custodied Takaful *Tabarru'* or microinsurance fund**.
6. **Rank 6: UC7: Identity-Linked Insurance Services / Portable KYC (`2S · 6C · 1W* · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC7 ranks sixth:** **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §9.1.4(ii)–(iv)** mandates NIN linkage and **explicitly directs insurtechs to *"consider Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) for authentication"*** (`C4 = S`), creating a direct regulatory hook for **Hyperledger Identus** W3C Verifiable Credentials within the 3-day KYC transmission window (**§9.2(iv)**). `C2` is scored **`W*` (`[Desk Evidence Insufficient: To Be Tested in M3]`)** because public sources do not establish whether Nigerian insurers will pay for portable cross-insurer VCs over single-insurer NIMC VSP API lookups (`C3 = C`), and `C5` is scored `C` pending verification of NIMC VSP integration and local Hyperledger Identus maintenance (Milestone 1 §1.3 & Appendix D).

### 4.3 Tier 3: Targeted Niche and Conditional Probes (`UC6`, `UC8`)

7. **Rank 7: UC6: Beneficiary Verification (`1S · 7C · 1W* · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC6 ranks seventh:** Mandatory employer Group Life (**NIIRA 2025 ss. 68–70**) and Annuity business (~44.3% of Life GWP in 2025) experience beneficiary verification friction (`C1 = C`), and PKI authentication is supported by **Insurtech Guidelines §9.1.4(iv)** (`C4 = C`). Because **UC6 shares the exact same Hyperledger Identus / Midnight Verifiable Credential and NIMC VSP stack as UC7** (`C3 = C`, `C5 = C`) and lacks standalone desk-stage demand evidence (`C2 = W*`), it is most efficiently tested in Milestone 3 as a Group-Life/Life module paired with UC7 and UC5.
8. **Rank 8: UC8: Stable-Value Settlement and Payment Rails (`0S · 4C · 5W · 0D` · `[Preliminary: Desk Evidence Only]`):**
   - **Why UC8 ranks eighth (and is retained per M1 §3.4 Rule 2):** Desk evidence shows that for **domestic retail insurance**, Nigeria's **NIBSS Instant Payments (NIP)** and PSB rails already settle fiat Naira in seconds at minimal cost without crypto friction (`C3 = W`). Simultaneously, on-chain stablecoin settlement faces **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §12.1(vi)** (prohibiting crypto premiums/claims without prior NAICOM approval), **§14.4** (instant insurer bank credit), **ISA 2025 s. 355** (SEC VASP licensing), **CBN's December 2023 VASP circular** (maximum two NGN settlement sweeps per quarter), and the **absence of native `cNGN` on Cardano** (`C4 = W`, `C5 = W`, `C7 = W`). Because §12.1(vi) is a prior-approval gate rather than an absolute statutory ban, UC8 is not scored `D`; it is retained as a targeted Tier 3 probe in Milestone 3 to test whether **VASP-at-the-edge fiat conversion for diaspora-funded policies or cross-border reinsurance (NIIRA s. 204(6))** is commercially and regulatorily viable.
