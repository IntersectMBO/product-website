## 1. Purpose, Epistemic Status and Protective Rules

This document executes Gate A, the pre-fieldwork provisional screen, and the Phase 3 infrastructure prerequisite matrix set out in Milestone 1 §3.4, Chapter 5 (§§5.1 to 5.6), §9.6 and Appendix D. It sits between desk review (Milestone 2) and primary fieldwork (Milestone 3).

Every score in this document is a tentative desk estimate derived from statutes, market bulletins, operator notices and academic literature. No stakeholder has been interviewed. Under Milestone 1 §5.3, a `Strong` rating requires *"clear evidence supporting viability; no significant barriers"*. Secondary sources cannot satisfy that threshold on operational, commercial or governance questions. **Zero cells out of 72 are rated `Strong` at Gate A.** Every use case enters Milestone 3 under Milestone 1's no-go default (§5.4) with low desk-only confidence.

### 1.1 Three Binding Rules From Milestone 1 §§3.4 and 6.3

1. **Fixed rubric, calibrated to desk evidence.** Under the Design Lock Rule (Milestone 1 §6.3), the nine criteria (`C1` to `C9`) and four ordinal levels (`Strong`, `Conditional`, `Weak`, `Disqualifying`) fixed in Milestone 1 §§5.2 and 5.3 cannot be altered. At Gate A, `Strong` is withheld across all criteria because even statutory penalties and survey gaps leave firm-level loss figures, switching costs and willingness to pay unmeasured. Criteria where secondary data show an open pathway with untested conditions are scored `Conditional (C)`. Criteria where strong non-blockchain incumbents or missing infrastructure weigh against a ledger are scored `Weak (W)`. Criteria that cannot be judged at all without primary interviews (`C2` demand and `C9` overall confidence) are marked `W*` (`Weak: evidence insufficient prior to Milestone 3`).
2. **Interview ordering only; no premature elimination.** Milestone 1 §3.4 states: *"Gate A may re-order interview emphasis but may not eliminate a candidate. All eight remain in the interview guides. The only exception is a Disqualifying finding grounded in primary legal text (for example, a statute that expressly prohibits the function), documented and open to challenge in Phase 5."* Because the [Preliminary Regulatory Scan](./regulatory-preliminary-scan.md) found conditional or sandbox routes rather than a blanket statutory prohibition on all architectures, **zero candidates receive `Disqualifying (D)` at Gate A**. Even UC8, where desk evidence already points towards rejection, stays in the Milestone 3 guides so regulators and VASPs can confirm or overturn that reading on the record.
3. **Clean-slate archival before Phase 6.** Milestone 1 §3.4 requires Gate A estimates to be archived once Milestone 3 interview time is allocated. They are never carried forward into the Phase 6 (Milestone 4) viability scorecard, which is scored afresh from primary and secondary evidence combined.

## 4. Cell-by-Cell Basis and Falsification Conditions (`UC1` to `UC8`)

### 4.1 Band 1: Institutional and Back-Office Hypotheses (`UC3`, `UC5`, `UC4`: 40% of Milestone 3 Depth)

**1. UC3: Shared claims and policy verification registry (`0S · 7C · 2W* · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why nothing is rated `Strong`:** NIIRA 2025 s. 207(1) mandates fraud reporting, but neither NAICOM nor the NIA publishes figures on duplicate claims across insurers (`C1 = C`), and no underwriter has been interviewed on willingness to fund a shared claims tool (`C2 = W*`, `C9 = W*`).
- **Why `C3` is `Conditional` rather than `Weak`:** The NIA already runs a central policy database (`NIID` / `askNIID` / `*565*11#` USSD) covering motor, marine and compulsory liability policies. If insurers are willing to let the NIA add a central claims table to `NIID`, a relational database wins on cost and simplicity and UC3 fails the blockchain necessity test. `C3` stays `Conditional` only because commercial insurers may refuse to upload client lists, claim amounts and renewal dates to a central industry server for fear of competitor poaching, or because independent adjusters and hospitals sit outside NIA membership (`C4 = C` pending NDPC cross-border views on hashes; `C5 = C` using L1 salted hashes; `C6 = C`; `C7 = C`; `C8 = C`).
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Upgraded only if NIA (`C05`), ILAN (`C07`) and non-sponsor claims heads (`NEM`, `AXA Mansard`, `Leadway`) confirm that competitive confidentiality blocks central `NIID` expansion. Downgraded to `No-Go` if NIA already plans or members prefer a conventional `NIID` claims API.

**2. UC5: Claims automation and 60-day settlement audit (`0S · 6C · 3W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why `C3` is `Weak`:** NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §12.1(iv) and NDPA 2023 s. 37 forbid unsupervised automated claim rejections. Web2 platforms (`Curacel`, `Octamile`) and NIBSS Instant Payments already automate post-admission payouts, while insurers' internal databases already undergo annual technology audits (§9.2(viii)). A public ledger adds value only if NAICOM's inspectorate treats independent timestamps of notification, admission and human escalation as stronger defence evidence in NIIRA s. 210(7) penalty disputes than an insurer's own log (`C1 = C`, `C2 = W*`, `C4 = C`, `C5 = C`, `C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = W*`).
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Tested directly with NAICOM (`A01`), FRCN (`A03`), ILAN (`C07`) and Curacel (`C08`). Rejected if NAICOM confirms internal audited logs suffice under s. 210.

**3. UC4: Reinsurance treaty reconciliation and bordereaux (`0S · 5C · 4W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why `C3` and `C8` are `Weak`:** NAICOM's online regulatory portal (`portal.naicom.gov.ng`) already handles s. 204 domestic-capacity and offshore-placement filings, Lagos-based regional reinsurers (`Africa Re`, `Continental Re`) run SAP FS-RI and DXC SICS, and the global `B3i` reinsurance blockchain failed in 2022 (`C3 = W`, `C8 = W`). `C4` to `C7` remain `Conditional` because NIIRA s. 204(6) removes in-person engagement for programme-compliant placements and smaller cedants/brokers still exchange spreadsheets (`C1 = C`, `C2 = W*`, `C9 = W*`).
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Tested with NAICOM's Reinsurance Directorate, `Continental Re`, `Africa Re`, `NCRIB` (`C06`) and `SCIB` (`C14`). Rejected if portal filings and bilateral messaging leave too little dispute cost to justify consortium governance.

### 4.2 Band 2: Retail, Takaful, Agricultural and Identity Hypotheses (`UC2`, `UC1`, `UC7`: 40% of Milestone 3 Depth)

**4. UC2: Microinsurance premium collection and Takaful pooling (`0S · 4C · 5W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why `C3`, `C5` and `C8` are `Weak`:** NIIRA 2025 s. 60(1) and Insurtech Guidelines §14.4 require premiums to credit a licensed insurer's bank account immediately, ruling out autonomous on-chain risk pools. A ledger can only mirror bank balances that licensed Takaful operators (`Salam`, `Noor`, `Jaiz`, `Cornerstone`) already segregate under Advisory Councils of Experts and external audit (`C3 = W`). On Cardano, the minimum UTxO deposit (`4,310 lovelace/byte`) and inactive protocol-level fee abstraction require operator-run daily Merkle batching and fiat fee sponsorship (`C5 = W`), while USSD policyholders cannot inspect a ledger (`C8 = W`). `C1`, `C4`, `C6` and `C7` are `Conditional`; `C2` and `C9` are `W*`.
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Tested with Takaful scholars (`C15`), NAICOM Takaful staff (`A01`), microinsurers (`Mutual Benefits`, `Casava`, `GOXI`) and Northern/Southern cooperatives (`D01`, `D02`). Rejected if existing bank segregation and Shariah audit leave no verification gap.

**5. UC1: Parametric agricultural and climate micro-insurance (`0S · 3C · 6W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why `C3`, `C5`, `C7` and `C8` are `Weak`:** `NAIC` (`B17`), `NIRSAL` (`D03`) and `Pula` (`C10`) already run area-yield and satellite index calculations off-chain, and executing an index on Plutus does nothing to reduce spatial basis risk across `NiMet`'s roughly 42 to 54 manned synoptic stations (`C3 = W`, `C8 = W`; Clarke, 2016). No Cardano oracle publishes Nigerian weather data, and `Orcfax` (`D14`) halted research, development and platform enhancement on 27 March 2026 with a contingency wind-down date of 31 July 2026 (`C5 = W`, `C7 = W`). `C1`, `C4` (§4.1(g) crop data and §5.0(vi) sandbox) and `C6` remain `Conditional`; `C2` and `C9` are `W*`.
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Tested with `NAIC`, `Leadway`, `Salam Takaful`, `Pula`, `NiMet` (`D04`), `Africa Re` and `AFAN` farmers (`D01`). Rejected if disputes stem from station density and subsidy arrears rather than distrust of off-chain trigger arithmetic.

**6. UC7: Identity-linked enrolment and reusable KYC (`0S · 2C · 7W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why five substantive criteria are `Weak`:** NIMC-licensed verification providers (`D05`: Smile ID, Seamfix, Prembly, QoreID) return NIN and BVN checks in seconds for roughly ₦10 to ₦100 per call (`C3 = W`), and the NIMC Act 2026 places National Public Key Infrastructure oversight under NIMC. Neither `Hyperledger Identus` nor `Veridian` (`D15`) has a published NIMC VSP connector (`C5 = W`, `C7 = W`), all three core gatekeepers (`NIMC A06`, `NDPC A07`, `VSPs D05`) are `Desk-Identified` (`C6 = W`), and annual policy renewal plus AML reliance liability under NIIRA s. 202 weaken cross-insurer credential reuse (`C8 = W`). Only `C1` and `C4` (§9.1.4(iv) PKI clause) are `Conditional`; `C2` and `C9` are `W*`.
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Survives only if microinsurers and embedded aggregators (`Casava`, `MyCover.ai`, `InsureIQ`) show that ₦50 to ₦100 API fees or rural offline gaps break ₦200 to ₦500 micro-policies and `NIMC`/`NAICOM` confirm lawful reliance.

### 4.3 Band 3: Constrained Niche Probes (`UC6`, `UC8`: 20% of Milestone 3 Depth)

**7. UC6: Beneficiary verification for life and group life (`0S · 2C · 7W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why five substantive criteria are `Weak`:** Formal employer group life (Pension Reform Act 2014 s. 4(5); NIIRA s. 68) runs through employers and pension fund administrators, and PenCom's circular effective 1 June 2025 removed prior Commission approval for death-benefit payouts (`C3 = W`). Above small-estate thresholds, wills, High Court probate grants, customary law and Islamic inheritance supersede any cryptographic credential, exposing an insurer that pays on a token alone to double-payment risk under Insurtech Guidelines §8.0(xvi) (`C4 = W`). Multi-decade policy horizons create severe device and key-loss risk for bereaved families (`C5 = W`, `C7 = W`, `C8 = W`). Only `C1` and `C6` (reachable life and Family Takaful underwriters) are `Conditional`; `C2` and `C9` are `W*`.
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Tested as a focused life/micro-life probe with `Heirs Life`, `AIICO`, `Leadway` and `Salam`/`Noor` Family Takaful to check whether any non-probate niche below the ₦2 million microinsurance cap remains.

**8. UC8: Stable-value settlement and payment rails (`0S · 1C · 8W · 0D`; Low desk confidence)**
- **Why six substantive criteria are `Weak`:** Domestically, NIBSS Instant Payments processed roughly 11 billion naira transactions in 2024 in seconds without FX or crypto friction, while `PAPSS` and CBN's 2024 IMTO naira-payout rule govern regional and inbound retail corridors (`C3 = W`). Cross-border or diaspora token settlement faces simultaneous gates under NAICOM §12.1(vi) and §14.4, SEC ISA 2025 s. 355 and CBN's two-sweep quarterly cap on VASP bank accounts (`C4 = W`). Regulated `cNGN` is absent from Cardano (`C5 = W`), `SEC`, `CBN`, `WrappedCBDC` and `Busha`/`Quidax` are all `Desk-Identified` (`C6 = W`), and conversion spreads plus multi-regulator approvals weigh against delivery and adoption (`C7 = W`, `C8 = W`). Only `C1` (cross-border reinsurance FX lag) is `Conditional`; `C2` and `C9` are `W*`.
- **Milestone 3 upgrade or rejection test:** Retained per Milestone 1 §3.4 Rule 2 solely to put three targeted questions to `NAICOM`, `SEC`/`CBN` contacts, reinsurers (`Continental Re`, `Africa Re`) and `WrappedCBDC`/`Busha`. Recommended for `No-Go` and `RFP 03` handoff unless a concrete 12 to 24 month approval path is confirmed.
