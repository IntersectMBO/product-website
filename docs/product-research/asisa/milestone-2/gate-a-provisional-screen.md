## 1. Purpose and Governance Rules of Gate A

This document executes **Gate A (The Pre-Fieldwork Provisional Screen)** and the Phase 3 **Infrastructure Prerequisite Assessment** as defined in **Section 3.4**, **Chapter 5 (`Sections 5.1–5.6`)**, **Section 9.6**, and **Appendix D** of the Milestone 1 Research Design Report. Gate A sits at the boundary between desk research, regulatory scanning, and ecosystem mapping (Milestone 2) and primary stakeholder fieldwork (Milestone 3).

### 1.1 Strict Enforcement of Milestone 1 Section 3.4 Protective Rules and Section 6.3 Design Lock

In accordance with the **Design Lock Rule** of Milestone 1 Section 6.3, Gate A applies the exact nine criteria (`C1`–`C9`), four-level ordinal scale (`Strong`, `Conditional`, `Weak`, `Disqualifying`), and decision rules locked at project inception. Three mandatory protective rules from **Milestone 1 Section 3.4** govern this provisional screen:

1. **Rule 1: Explicit Early Desk-Guess Labelling:** Every criterion score and provisional tier in this document is an initial desk estimate prior to primary interviews and is explicitly labelled **`[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`**. Before any stakeholder has been interviewed, rating operational, architectural, or adoption criteria as validated strengths would overstate desk evidence. At Gate A, **`Strong (S)` is restricted strictly to Criterion 1 (`Problem severity`) where primary statutes or national survey datasets directly document an acute market failure on paper**, whereas all criteria requiring empirical stakeholder verification (`C2` through `C9`) are scored no higher than **`Conditional (C)`** (or **`Weak (W)`** where desk barriers or missing data exist).
2. **Rule 2: Re-Ordering of Interview Emphasis Without Premature Elimination:** *"Gate A may re-order interview emphasis but may not eliminate a candidate. All eight remain in the interview guides. The only exception is a Disqualifying finding grounded in primary legal text (for example, a statute that expressly prohibits the function), documented and open to challenge in Phase 5."* Because the Preliminary Regulatory Scan ([Preliminary Regulatory Scan](./regulatory-preliminary-scan.md)) indicates that all eight candidate use cases possess legally permissible pathways under the *Nigerian Insurance Industry Reform Act (NIIRA) 2025*, the *NAICOM Guidelines for Insurtech Operations in Nigeria (2025)*, the *Nigeria Data Protection Act (NDPA) 2023*, and the *Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025* when structured within the statutory constraints identified in the [Preliminary Regulatory Scan](./regulatory-preliminary-scan.md), **zero candidate use cases receive a `Disqualifying (D)` score at Gate A, and all eight hypotheses (`UC1`–`UC8`) advance to Milestone 3 primary validation**.
3. **Rule 3: Clean-Slate Archival Prior to Phase 6:** *"Gate A scores are archived and never carried into the Phase 6 scorecard."* Once Milestone 3 interview allocation is set, these early desk guesses are archived so that the final Phase 6 (Milestone 4) viability screen is scored from a clean slate grounded in triangulated primary and secondary evidence.

### Four-Level Ordinal Scoring Scale at Gate A (Milestone 1 Section 5.3 — Applied to Early Desk Estimates)

- **`Strong (S)`:** Clear primary statutory or national survey evidence on paper (at Gate A prior to interviews, restricted strictly to **Criterion 1: Problem severity** where statutory penalties or national datasets document the problem; never assigned to `C2`–`C9` before primary interviews).
- **`Conditional (C)`:** Early desk guess indicating a plausible pathway subject to regulatory, technical, governance, or commercial conditions that remain unverified until Milestone 3 stakeholder interviews.
- **`Weak (W)`:** Desk evidence is either insufficient prior to primary fieldwork (`W* [Desk Evidence Insufficient: To Be Tested in M3]`) or identifies significant operational, regulatory, infrastructure, or non-blockchain baseline barriers (`W [Significant Desk Barrier Identified]`).
- **`Disqualifying (D)`:** A structural barrier that cannot be resolved within 12 to 24 months (at Gate A, restricted strictly to a primary-statute Hard Prohibition per Milestone 1 Section 3.4 Rule 2).

## 4. Analytical Justification of Gate A Provisional Scores (`UC1`–`UC8`)

### 4.1 Tier 1: Primary Fieldwork Emphasis (`UC3`, `UC4`, `UC5`)

1. **Rank 1: UC3: Fraud-Resistant Shared Claims Registry (`1S · 8C · 0W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale and comparison against NIA's central `NIID` database:** **NIIRA 2025 Section 207(1)** imposes a statutory duty on insurers to report suspected fraudulent insurance conduct (`C1 = S`). However, `C2` through `C9` are rated **`Conditional (C)`** as early desk guesses because the Nigerian Insurers Association (NIA) already operates a centralised database, the **Nigerian Insurance Industry Database (NIID)** (alongside the Nigerian Insurance Industry Portal, `NIIP`), which verifies active motor third-party policies and marine certificates. If Nigerian underwriters are willing to expand `NIID` into a multi-line claims database hosted by the NIA and trust a single central administrator with plain-text or centrally encrypted claims records, **a conventional centralised SQL/API database would be simpler and cheaper than a blockchain (failing Wüst & Gervais's 2018 necessity test)**. A Cardano or Midnight cryptographic layer would only perform better than `NIID` (`C3 = C`) if Milestone 3 interviews confirm three structural conditions that `NIID` does not currently address: (i) *cross-line claims scope vs active-policy lookup*, since `NIID` primarily checks whether a motor policy is active at roadside or port inspection rather than tracking cross-company duplicate claims submissions and loss-adjuster payouts across health, property/fire, goods-in-transit, life, and comprehensive motor lines; (ii) *competitor commercial confidentiality ("client-poaching" concern)*, where competing underwriters resist uploading granular comprehensive claims, corporate client identities, and policy renewal dates into a plain-text central database accessible to industry rivals or third-party administrators, whereas salted `Blake2b-256` hash commitments (`VIN/Asset ID + Loss Date + Claim Type`) or zero-knowledge proofs allow duplicate-claim collision detection without exposing proprietary customer lists unless a match occurs; and (iii) *multi-party non-NIA auditability and NDPA 2023 erasure compliance*, where claims verification involves actors outside the NIA's member-underwriter perimeter (independent loss adjusters, hospitals/HMOs, auto-repair workshops, police/FRSC reports, and NAICOM auditors) and an append-only hash log with off-chain domestic PII storage avoids creating a central honeypot of sensitive personal or medical data under **NDPA 2023 ss. 24, 30, and 34(1)(d)** (`C4 = C`, `C5 = C`). Whether these three potential advantages justify a blockchain over an upgraded `NIID` central database is an unverified desk hypothesis to be tested directly with the NIA (`C05`) and underwriting claims heads in Milestone 3 (`C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`).
2. **Rank 2: UC4: Reinsurance Treaty Reconciliation (`0S · 9C · 0W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** All nine criteria for UC4 are scored **`Conditional (C)`** as early desk guesses prior to interviews (`0S · 9C`). On paper, **NIIRA 2025 Section 204(6)** removes the requirement for in-person engagement on approved foreign reinsurance placements under the annual reinsurance programme submitted to NAICOM, and **Section 60(5)** exempts reinsurance contracts from retail prepayment rules (`C4 = C`). While multi-party state synchronisation across cedants, reinsurance brokers, and treaty reinsurers is a candidate for shared ledger reconciliation and Plutus execution fees (~0.25–0.50 ADA) are small relative to wholesale treaty balances (`C1 = C`, `C3 = C`), the **B3i consortium insolvency (L06)** demonstrates that reinsurance DLT initiatives fail when underwriters resist core-ERP migration or prefer standard ACORD/EDI bilateral portals (`C2 = C`, `C5 = C`, `C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`). Milestone 3 interviews with Leadway, NSIA, Continental Re, Africa Re, and NCRIB brokers will test whether treaty reconciliation friction is severe enough to justify a shared cryptographic bordereaux layer.
3. **Rank 3: UC5: Claims Automation / Smart Adjudication (`1S · 8C · 0W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** Slow and disputed claims settlement is documented as a primary driver of Nigeria's insurance trust deficit (EFInA, 2023), and **NIIRA 2025 Section 210(3) and (7)** subjects insurers to a **60-day settlement deadline**, a ₦500,000 fine plus **monthly compound interest at the prevailing bank rate**, and direct deduction from CBN statutory deposits (`C1 = S`). All remaining criteria (`C2`–`C9`) are rated **`Conditional (C)`** as early desk guesses: (a) **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §12.1(iv)** and **NDPA 2023 s. 37** prohibit unsupervised automated claim rejections without human intervention, restricting smart contracts to post-admission payout triggers and 60-day SLA compliance logging while escalating all potential denials to a human adjuster (`C4 = C`); and (b) Milestone 3 interviews must test whether on-chain SLA timestamping provides sufficient audit value over established Web2 claims-automation platforms such as **Curacel** (`C2 = C`, `C3 = C`, `C5 = C`, `C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`).

### 4.2 Tier 2: High-Impact Retail, Agricultural, Takaful, and Identity Emphasis (`UC2`, `UC1`, `UC7`)

4. **Rank 4: UC2: Microinsurance Premium Collection and Pooling, Including Takaful (`1S · 8C · 0W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** Retail exclusion stands at 97% of Nigerian adults (EFInA, 2023), and distrust of pooled fund management is a documented barrier across informal and Takaful segments (`C1 = S`). However, `C2` through `C9` are rated **`Conditional (C)`** as early desk guesses: (a) **NIIRA 2025 Section 60(1)** ("No Premium, No Cover") and **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §14.4** (requiring instant credit of premiums to the licensed insurer's bank account) prohibit unlicensed autonomous DeFi risk pools (`C4 = C`); and (b) Cardano's `4,310 lovelace/byte` min-UTxO deposit and ~0.17 ADA base fee make unbatched per-transaction L1 micro-premiums uneconomic against NIBSS/PSB fiat rails (`C3 = C`, `C5 = C`). Milestone 3 fieldwork will test whether Takaful operators and microinsurers value a daily-batched cryptographic audit rail mirroring a bank-custodied *Tabarru'* or microinsurance fund (`C2 = C`, `C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`).
5. **Rank 5: UC1: Parametric Agricultural Insurance (`1S · 7C · 1W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** Smallholder climate vulnerability across >35 million farming households is well documented (`C1 = S`), and **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §4.1(g)** permits Partnering Insurtechs to conduct *"crop cutting, data collection and yield calculation"* alongside the **§5.0(vi) Regulatory Sandbox** (`C4 = C`). However, Criterion 5 (`Infrastructure readiness`) is scored **`W`** at desk stage because **neither Charli3 nor Orcfax currently provides calibrated Nigerian agro-meteorological feeds (NiMet ground stations or CHIRPS/NDVI satellite indices)** on Cardano mainnet, requiring a bespoke oracle adapter. Additionally, **Clarke (2016)** shows that spatial basis risk suppresses index demand unless paired with multi-scale yield verification and input-credit bundling (`C2 = C`, `C3 = C`), and **NAICOM §12.1(vi)** requires payouts to settle in fiat Naira over NIBSS/PSB rails (`C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`).
6. **Rank 6: UC7: Identity-Linked Insurance Services / Portable KYC (`0S · 8C · 1W* · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §9.1.4(ii)–(iv)** mandates NIN linkage and instructs insurtechs to *"consider Public Key Infrastructure (PKI) for authentication"* (`C4 = C`), offering a potential regulatory hook for **Hyperledger Identus** W3C Verifiable Credentials within the 3-day KYC transmission window (**§9.2(iv)**). However, `C1` and `C3`–`C9` are scored **`Conditional (C)`** and `C2` is scored **`W*` (`[Desk Evidence Insufficient: To Be Tested in M3]`)** because desk sources do not establish whether Nigerian insurers will pay for portable cross-insurer VCs over standard single-insurer NIMC VSP API lookups (such as Smile ID or Seamfix).

### 4.3 Tier 3: Targeted Niche and Conditional Probes (`UC6`, `UC8`)

7. **Rank 7: UC6: Beneficiary Verification (`0S · 8C · 1W* · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale:** Mandatory employer Group Life (**NIIRA 2025 ss. 68–70**) and Annuity lines experience beneficiary verification friction (`C1 = C`), and PKI authentication is referenced in **Insurtech Guidelines §9.1.4(iv)** (`C4 = C`). Because **UC6 shares the same Hyperledger Identus / Midnight Verifiable Credential and NIMC VSP stack as UC7** (`C3 = C`, `C5 = C`, `C6 = C`, `C7 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`) and lacks standalone desk-stage demand evidence (`C2 = W*`), it is evaluated in Milestone 3 as a Group-Life/Life probe paired with UC7 and UC5.
8. **Rank 8: UC8: Stable-Value Settlement and Payment Rails (`0S · 4C · 5W · 0D` · `[Early Desk Guess — Unverified Prior to Interviews]`):**
   - **Desk rationale (retained per M1 §3.4 Rule 2):** Desk evidence indicates that for **domestic retail insurance**, Nigeria's **NIBSS Instant Payments (NIP)** and PSB rails already settle fiat Naira in seconds at low cost without crypto friction (`C3 = W`). Simultaneously, on-chain stablecoin settlement faces **NAICOM Insurtech Guidelines §12.1(vi)** (prohibiting crypto premiums/claims without prior NAICOM approval), **§14.4** (instant insurer bank credit), **ISA 2025 s. 355** (SEC VASP licensing), **CBN's December 2023 VASP circular** (maximum two NGN settlement sweeps per quarter), and the **absence of native `cNGN` on Cardano** (`C4 = W`, `C5 = W`, `C7 = W`). Because §12.1(vi) is a prior-approval gate rather than an absolute statutory ban, UC8 is not scored `D`; it is retained as a targeted Tier 3 probe in Milestone 3 to test whether **VASP-at-the-edge fiat conversion for diaspora-funded policies or cross-border reinsurance (NIIRA s. 204(6))** is viable (`C1 = C`, `C2 = W*`, `C6 = C`, `C8 = C`, `C9 = C`).
