# Regional Market Prioritisation Matrix and Infrastructure Prerequisite Assessment

This report evaluates the eight candidate country clusters established in Milestone 1 [Candidate Regions](../milestone-1/candidate-regions.md) against the ten-dimension Regional Adoption Readiness Framework (RARF) in [Adoption Readiness](../milestone-1/research-design-document/adoption-readiness.md) and the advancement gates in [Region Selection](../milestone-1/research-design-document/region-selection.md) [`REF-72`]. East Asia remains outside the scope of this study, as established in Milestone 1.

All provisional scores, advancement classifications, and infrastructure ratings reflect Phase 2 desk research and are structured for empirical testing and potential falsification during Phase 3 fieldwork (Weeks 7–12).

---

## 1. Methodological Approach and Desk-Stage Calibration

Desk research can verify enacted statutes, central bank circulars, domestic payment-switch volumes, and public on-chain liquidity. It cannot establish whether a specific agency or enterprise holds live pilot budget authority, whether a local systems integrator can sustain production service levels, or how quickly a compliance team will clear a new settlement rail. To keep that evidentiary boundary visible across all 80 cells of the RARF matrix, Phase 2 applies four scoring conventions:

1. **Secondary Evidence Ceiling:** On the 1-to-5 RARF rubric, a top score of 5 requires primary confirmation of operational commitment and verified institutional capacity. Prior to interviews, dimensions observable through official statistics and statutory texts (`D1` Problem Severity, `D2` Use-Case Fit, `D4` Regulatory Readiness, `D5` Infrastructure Readiness, `D6` Stablecoin and Settlement Readiness, and `D8` Existing Ecosystem Presence) are capped at 4; a rating of 5 is reserved for post-fieldwork revision in Milestone 4.
2. **Conservative Cap on Fieldwork-Dependent Dimensions (`D3`, `D7`, `D9`, `D10`):** Four dimensions require direct primary validation and are capped at 3 at the desk stage:
   - **Counterparty Accessibility (`D3`):** Rated up to 3. Although the research team holds institutional networks in Buenos Aires, Nairobi, Lagos, and Barcelona (`Tier 2: Warm Access` and `Tier 3: Referred`), those contacts remain pre-interview recruitment channels until primary sessions take place.
   - **Delivery Capacity (`D7`):** Rated up to 3 pending technical assessment of local software cooperatives and systems integrators through the [Delivery Partners Question Pack](../milestone-1/research-design-document/question-packs/delivery-partners.md).
   - **Adoption Signal Strength (`D9`):** Rated up to 3 under the Milestone 1 [Adoption Signals](../milestone-1/research-design-document/adoption-signals.md) standard, which reserves `CASS Tier 1` (Verified Adoption) and `CASS Tier 2` (Strong Signal) for primary confirmation of institutional ownership and resource allocation. To ensure uniform desk calibration, `D9 = 3` is assigned only where a cluster exhibits at least two independent `CASS Tier 3` Cardano-linked ecosystem, RWA pool, or institutional/academic R&D footprints (`RealFi USDrf` + `World Mobile` + `Empowa` in East Africa; `UBA/UTN` formal Cardano academic agreements + `AtixLabs` Cardano engineering PoCs in Southern Cone), whereas clusters with zero or a single isolated Cardano R&D prototype (`Brazil`: single Sept 2026 `PUC-Rio` prototype; `West Africa` and `Iberia`: zero live Cardano institutional prototypes, with summits and events scored at `Tier 4`) receive `D9 = 2`. Non-Cardano deployments (`QuarkID` on `ZKsync`, scored under `D5`) and non-Cardano statutory drivers (`EUDR`, scored under `D1/D2`) are excluded from `D9` credit.
   - **Time-to-Pilot Feasibility (`D10`):** Rated up to 3 pending primary verification of procurement cycles and compliance sign-off timelines.
3. **Weighting Schedule and Desk Ceiling:** Under the standard RARF weights (`D1`: 15%, `D2`: 15%, `D3`: 15%, `D4`: 10%, `D5`: 10%, `D6`: 10%, `D7`: 10%, `D8`: 5%, `D9`: 5%, `D10`: 5%; total = 100%), these caps produce a maximum theoretical desk composite of 3.65 / 5.00 (73.0 / 100). Table 1 reports both the weighted composite and the unweighted ten-dimension mean so readers can compare cluster rankings under either specification.
4. **Advancement Classifications, Hub Anchoring, and Interview Allocation:**
   - **`Conditional-Go` (`>= 3.00` Composite & `>= 6/10` Dimensions `>= 3.0` per Milestone 1 Region Selection §8.3, subject to all five gates):** Clearing the `>= 3.00` composite and `>= 6/10` dimension breadth threshold is necessary but not sufficient for advancement. A cluster enters Phase 3 only when it clears all five Milestone 1 advancement criteria — scores of 3.0 or higher on at least six of ten dimensions (Gate A), an identifiable 12–24 month counterparty pathway backed by an active regional hub (Gate B), a specific testable use case (Gate C), independent problem-severity data (Gate D), and no blocking regulatory or infrastructure constraint (Gate E).
   - **`Monitor` (2.50–2.99 Desk Band, plus `>= 3.00` clusters overridden by Gate B, Gate C, Gate D, or Gate E):** Exhibits market scale or statutory clarity, but fails an individual advancement gate — such as domestic incumbent saturation (`Brazil`), high capital floors (`Brazil`), regulatory licensing gaps (`Andean`, `Mexico`), low acute retail payment friction (`Iberia D1 = 2` under Gate D), or absence of local field hub coverage (Gate B). Held at the desk stage (0 Phase 3 interviews). This gate-override rule explains why Iberia (3.15) and Brazil (3.00) remain on `Monitor`.
   - **`No-Go` (below 2.50):** Faces an active central bank or statutory prohibition that precludes compliant pilot delivery within 12–24 months. Excluded from Phase 3 fieldwork.
   - **Resource Split and Regional Hub Allocation:** Operationalising Milestone 1 [Region Selection](../milestone-1/research-design-document/region-selection.md) (~60% of primary research capacity to the two highest-scoring clusters and ~40% to remaining priority coverage) across the three advancing emerging-market hubs (3 Local Project Teams per hub = 9 Local Teams) allocates 14–17 interviews to **Priority 1: Southern Cone** (Buenos Aires Hub) and 13–16 interviews to **Priority 2: East Africa** (Nairobi Hub) — 27–33 combined (~64%) — alongside 15–18 interviews to **Priority 3: West Africa** (Lagos Hub, comprising 14–16 interviews across `H1`/`H2` plus 1–2 diagnostic falsification calls on `H3` Land Registry, ~36%). Each hub anchors regional outreach to neighbouring cluster markets (Buenos Aires to Uruguay and Paraguay; Nairobi to Rwanda and Uganda; Lagos to the paired Accra/Ghana corridor). Table 3 tests how cluster rankings shift when hub-access weights are removed.

## 4. Regional Market Narratives

The narratives below set out the desk evidence behind each cluster's scores, gate verdict, and Phase 3 scope.

### 4.1 Southern Cone (`Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay`) — `3.30 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 1)

Chronic peso inflation and multi-tier foreign-exchange spreads make Argentina the most stablecoin-intensive market in Latin America, with stablecoins accounting for over 60% of national crypto volume [`REF-37`, `REF-56`]. Over the past three years, statutory rules for virtual-asset commerce have moved from informal tolerance to formal licensing. In Argentina, Executive Decree DNU 70/2023 amended Articles 765 and 766 of the Civil and Commercial Code [`REF-32`], permitting private parties to denominate and settle commercial obligations in foreign currencies or virtual assets. Law 27.739 and National Securities Commission (CNV) General Resolution 1172/2026 (published 15 October 2026, replacing GR 1058/2025 in full) [`REF-31`] now govern more than 100 registered Virtual Asset Service Providers (PSAVs), imposing USD 35,000-150,000 net-worth floors, custody segregation, monthly volume and custody reporting (effective November 2026), and a ban on crypto mixers. Across the river, Uruguay enacted Law 20.345 and BCU Circular 2507 [`REF-39`], bringing virtual-asset providers under Central Bank (BCU/SSF) supervision. The binding constraint in Argentina sits at the banking perimeter: Central Bank (BCRA) Communications "A" 7506 and "A" 7759 — remaining in force in BCRA's consolidated *Texto Ordenado* through November 2026 [`REF-33`] — bar commercial banks and retail payment service providers (PSPs) from holding crypto on their own balance sheets. Registered PSAVs (Lemon Cash with 4M-4.5M+ users, Belo, Ripio, Fiwind) therefore rely on licensed third-party banking APIs (BIND) to connect virtual assets to the national Transferencias 3.0 interoperable QR network (CVU/CBU) [`REF-33`, `REF-37`].

At the municipal and institutional layer, the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires (GCBA) operates Latin America's largest public self-sovereign identity deployment (QuarkID / miBA, serving more than 3.6M residents), alongside active university (UBA, UTN) and technology cooperative (FACTTIC) networks [`REF-36`, `REF-38`]. Incumbent lock-in is nonetheless deep: QuarkID anchors its primary state hashes on ZKsync Era (Ethereum L2) [`REF-36`], consumer stablecoin cards settle predominantly on Tron TRC-20 and Polygon, and domestic peso payments are already covered by Transferencias 3.0 and Mercado Pago [`REF-33`, `REF-37`]. Under the `>= 2` Cardano-linked `Tier 3` signal rule (§1), Southern Cone's `D9 = 3` rests on `UBA`/`UTN` formal Cardano academic agreements and `AtixLabs`/cooperative Cardano engineering PoCs. Phase 3 fieldwork anchored by the **Buenos Aires Hub** (14–17 planned interviews across 3 Local Teams, extending outreach into Uruguay and Paraguay) therefore narrows onto two multi-party niches where existing rails leave operational friction: B2B cooperative and agribusiness programmable USD contract escrow under Article 765 CCyC (`H6`), and cross-border Mercosur (AR-UY-PY) professional credential verification and contractor settlement (`H7`). Interviews will test whether registered Argentine PSAVs will integrate Cardano USDCx or USDM settlement APIs and whether QuarkID maintainers will add `did:prism` (Hyperledger Identus / Veridian) or `did:plc` DID resolution alongside ZKsync.

### 4.2 East Africa (`Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda`) — `3.15 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 2)

East Africa combines mature mobile-money distribution with two external commercial pressures: European Union export compliance rules for smallholder agriculture and 5.5%–8.0% cross-border East African Community (EAC) remittance costs [`REF-22`, `REF-55`]. Statutory clarity across the cluster improved markedly in 2025–2026. Kenya's Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASP) Act, 2025 (Act No. 20 of 2025) entered into force on 2025-11-04, splitting licensing between the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) and the Capital Markets Authority (CMA) [`REF-16`], while the Finance Act 2025 repealed the 3% gross Digital Asset Tax in favour of a 10% excise duty on VASP platform service fees (effective 2025-07-01) [`REF-17`]. Rwanda enacted Law No. 023/2026 of 25/05/2026 Regulating Virtual Asset Business (published 28 May 2026), naming CMA Rwanda as lead licensing authority with BNR co-oversight, regulating 100%-collateralised stablecoins (Art. 19), prohibiting direct virtual-asset payments without BNR approval (Art. 15(3)), and barring PII or sovereign-asset tokenisation (Art. 17(1)) [`REF-23`]. Uganda (BoU NPS sandbox / FIA) complements the cluster as a corridor market [`REF-24`].

Within Kenya, Safaricom's M-Pesa (41.68M active monthly users in FY26; 42.3M in H1 FY26/27; ~89% of Kenya's mobile-money subscriptions within ~91% adult penetration) [`REF-20`] and the government's eCitizen portal (13.6M+ users, 16,000–21,000+ services) [`REF-21`] already handle domestic Kenyan shilling retail payments and central fee collection. Multi-party verification gaps remain in agricultural export supply chains and cross-border or humanitarian cash-based transfers. Under the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR, Reg. (EU) 2023/1115 as amended by Reg. (EU) 2025/2650), binding compliance starts on 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators (30 June 2027 for micro and small enterprises), requiring the first EU operator to file Due Diligence Statements backed by farm-plot GPS coordinates (points `<4 ha`, polygons `>4 ha`) and deforestation-free custody chains across hundreds of independent cooperatives, millers, and exporters [`REF-22`]. Agritech ERPs (iProcure, Apollo Agriculture, KTDA) manage internal data in SQL databases, leaving European importers and auditors without shared cross-actor provenance proofs. On payment rails, aggregators such as Kotani Pay bridge stablecoins into M-Pesa over USSD (`*483#`) across 15+ networks, though 95%+ of commercial volume settles on Celo (MiniPay, 20M+ wallets), Tron, Polygon, and Stellar [`REF-25`, `REF-58`]. East Africa's provisional `D9 = 3` reflects multiple independent Cardano-linked `Tier 3` footprints (`RealFi USDrf` `~USD 5.60M`, `World Mobile`, and `Empowa`). Phase 3 fieldwork anchored by the **Nairobi Hub** (13–16 planned interviews across 3 Local Teams, covering Kigali and Kampala alongside Nairobi) will test Hyperledger Identus cooperative credentials and `CIP-0020` batch lot anchoring — designed around Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019 (Sections 48–49 cross-border safeguards and Section 50 / Reg. 26 local serving-copy rules for strategic state sectors [`REF-18`], subject to local counsel review under `LC-07`) — and assess what liquidity terms would activate Cardano USDCx or USDM on Kenyan USSD/M-Pesa ramps.

### 4.3 West Africa (`Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal`) — `3.15 / 5.00` (`Conditional-Go`, Priority Playbook 3)

Nigeria and Ghana anchor Sub-Saharan Africa's largest digital-asset and remittance corridor (USD 205B+ regional Sub-Saharan inflows in Chainalysis 2025, with Nigeria at USD 92.1B, and USD 101B regional inflows under Chainalysis's revised 2026 methodology) [`REF-56`], facing average regional remittance costs of ~8.46% (up to 8.78% quarterly) [`REF-55`]. Regulatory frameworks in both Anglophone anchors moved from restriction to statutory licensing between December 2023 and January 2026. In Nigeria, CBN Circular FPR/DIR/PUB/CIR/002/003 (22 Dec 2023) lifted the 2021 banking ban on VASP settlement accounts [`REF-03`]; the Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025 codified virtual assets under SEC Nigeria alongside the ARIP onboarding cohort (Busha, Quidax) [`REF-01`, `REF-02`]; and SEC Circular No. 26-1 (16 Jan 2026) raised minimum paid-up capital for VASPs to NGN 300M–2.0B (DAX/Custodian: NGN 2.0B; DAOP/RATOP: NGN 1.0B; DAI/DAPO: NGN 500M; AVASP: NGN 300M; compliance deadline 30 June 2027) [`REF-01`]. In Ghana, Parliament enacted the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154) in December 2025, establishing dual BoG and SEC Ghana licensing [`REF-12`]. Senegal remains inside the restrictive BCEAO / AMF-UMOA (formerly CREPMF) banking union and is held as a secondary desk sub-market [`REF-15`].

Domestic Web2 infrastructure and competing chains narrow where Cardano can add value. For local-currency retail transfers, Nigeria's instant interbank switch (NIBSS NIP, ~11.2B transactions worth NGN 1.07 quadrillion in 2024 alongside the 2026 National Payment Stack rollout) [`REF-10`] and Ghana's GhIPSS GIP and MTN MoMo [`REF-14`] already settle near-instantly, while Tron TRC-20 USDT and Celo MiniPay (20M+ global activations) dominate retail dollar wallets [`REF-58`]. Nigeria's regulated Naira stablecoin cNGN (WrappedCBDC Ltd, natively on Bantu plus Ethereum, BNB Chain, Polygon, Tron, and Base) holds USD 0.00 supply on Cardano [`REF-11`, `REF-59`], and CBN rules require Naira-only retail IMTO payouts [`REF-04`]. In identity and public records, single-authority verification runs through NIMC's NIN (136M+ records) via licensed VSPs (Smile ID, Seamfix, Prembly) [`REF-09`] and the Nigerian Insurers Association's NIID (askNIID / `*565*11#`) [`REF-10`]. Meanwhile, land transfers under Nigeria's Land Use Act 1978 s. 22 require discretionary Governor's Consent inside state GIS databases (Lagos e-GIS, Kaduna KADGIS) [`REF-07`]. Anchoring West Africa in **Lagos Hub** alongside the paired Accra/Ghana corridor reflects Nigeria's statutory and commercial scale within the cluster while sustaining cross-border Ghana coverage. Phase 3 fieldwork across 3 Local Teams (15–18 planned interviews — 14–16 across `H1`/`H2` plus 1–2 diagnostic `H3` calls) will test wholesale B2B and inter-MTO cross-border liquidity with well-capitalised SEC-regulated VASPs (`H1`), multi-issuer academic and trade-guild verifiable credentials (`H2`), and state land-registry lock-in (`H3`).

### 4.4 Iberia (`Spain - Barcelona`) — `3.15 / 5.00` (`Monitor: European Reference Benchmark`)

Spain exhibits low domestic payment friction (`D1` = 2) because SEPA Instant and Bizum (~31M–32M users, 1.1B+ operations in 2025) already provide instant, zero-fee euro transfers [`REF-54`]. Iberia's relevance to the research portfolio lies in its statutory clarity under EU MiCA (Reg. (EU) 2023/1114; Spain's shortened transitional window closed 30 December 2025) [`REF-49`] and EU eIDAS 2.0 (Reg. (EU) 2024/1183) [`REF-50`]. At the municipal procurement layer, Article 118 of Spain's Public Sector Contracts Law (LCSP Ley 9/2017) allows agencies, public foundations, and universities to award direct minor service contracts (Contratos Menores) under EUR 15,000 (excl. VAT) without an open EU tender [`REF-53`]. At the same time, Real Decreto-ley 14/2019 bars public administrations from using distributed ledgers for statutory citizen identification pending special state security regulations [`REF-51`], and EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 (final version 2.0 adopted 7 July 2026) prohibit placing personal data or linkable identifiers on public blockchains [`REF-52`]. Because statutory tenders sit on PLACSP/PSCP, citizen ID runs on Cl@ve/idCAT, and domestic payment friction is low, Iberia remains on desk monitoring (0 Phase 3 interviews) as a European regulatory benchmark.

### 4.5 Non-Advancing Clusters Held on `Monitor` (`Brazil`, `Andean`, `Mexico`) and `No-Go` (`Horn / Ethiopia`)

Four remaining clusters do not advance to Phase 3 fieldwork. Three (`Brazil`, `Andean`, `Mexico`) remain on desk `Monitor`, and `Horn / Ethiopia` is classified as `No-Go`:

- **Brazil (3.00 / 5.00 — `Monitor`):** Brazil ranked #1 globally in Chainalysis's 2026 Global Crypto Adoption Index (USD 252.5B on-chain activity) [`REF-56`] and enacted Law 14.478/2022 [`REF-41`]. Two structural barriers nonetheless weigh against near-term Cardano entry without a local hub. First, BCB Pix (187.8M+ users; 319.7M single-day transfer record on 6 Nov 2026) saturates domestic retail payments, and BCB Drex pivoted away from DLT in late 2025 [`REF-42`]. Second, BCB Resolutions 519, 520, and 521/2025 (effective 2026-02-02) paired with Joint CMN/BCB Resolution No. 14 set BRL 10.8 million to BRL 37.2 million paid-in capital and net equity floors on SPSAVs, while Resolution 521/2025 folds cross-border stablecoin transfers into Brazil's Foreign Exchange (Câmbio) regime with a USD 100,000 cap on non-FX-authorised counterparties and mandatory self-hosted wallet tracing [`REF-41`]. Institutional BRL stablecoins (Braza BBRL on XRPL/Polygon; Transfero BRZ on Ethereum/Solana/Polygon/BNB/Avalanche) hold zero Cardano supply [`REF-43`, `REF-59`]. Although the Cardano Foundation, Petrobras, and PUC-Rio unveiled a research-stage SAF / Diesel R traceability prototype in September 2026 (`CASS-M2-10`, `Tier 3`), no commercial production rollout exists yet [`REF-67`].
- **Andean (Colombia, Peru — 2.60 / 5.00 — `Monitor`):** Domestic retail rails (Yape at ~16.7M MAU and Plin in Peru; Nequi at 20M+, DaviPlata, and Bre-B launched mid-2025 in Colombia) cover everyday local transfers [`REF-46`–`REF-48`], while dollar savings apps (Littio) run on Polygon and Avalanche [`REF-47`]. In Colombia, Draft PSAV Bill No. 510/2025 was archived in the Senate, leaving a statutory VASP vacuum after LaArenera expired [`REF-46`]; Peru's SBS Res. 02648-2024 covers AML and Travel Rule registration only [`REF-48`]. Without a local field hub in Bogota or Lima, Andean stays on `Monitor`.
- **Mexico (2.55 / 5.00 — `Monitor`):** Banxico recorded USD 61.79B in 2025 remittances (USD 41.80B YTD Aug 2026) [`REF-45`], yet Bitso Business already processed 14.7% of all US-Mexico remittances in Jan-Aug 2026 directly into Banxico SPEI [`REF-45`]. Banxico Circular 4/2019 also bars regulated ITFs and banks from client-facing virtual-asset operations [`REF-44`]. Because Cardano's global stablecoin supply (~USD 59.6M) is a small fraction of Bitso's corridor flow [`REF-59`] and the research team holds no local hub in Mexico, Mexico scores >= 3.0 on only 4 of 10 dimensions and remains on `Monitor`.
- **Horn / Ethiopia (2.00 / 5.00 — `No-Go`):** Despite acute FX pressure, the NBE Public Notice of May 2022 and Proclamation No. 1282/2023 prohibit cryptocurrency settlement [`REF-26`]. The July 2024 Birr float (Directive FXD/01/2024) confines formal remittances to NBE-licensed banks, EthSwitch, and Telebirr (over 60M users) [`REF-27`, `REF-30`]. In digital identity and education (`H14`, `H15`), Proclamation No. 1284/2023 and World Bank P179040 (USD 350M) consolidated national ID on non-blockchain Fayda (MOSIP, ~39M-50M enrolled) [`REF-28`], alongside Proclamation 1321/2024 and INSA Proclamation 808/2013 [`REF-29`]. Ethiopia is therefore excluded from near-term operational entry.
