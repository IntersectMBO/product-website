# Preliminary Regulatory and Compliance Scan

This report examines the statutory, regulatory, data-protection, foreign-exchange, and public-procurement rules across all eight candidate regional clusters evaluated in Phase 2. It establishes the legal baseline for Phase 3 stakeholder interviews conducted under the Milestone 1 [Regulators and Policy Question Pack](../milestone-1/research-design-document/question-packs/regulators.md) [`REF-72`].

All enacted statutes, central bank circulars, tax reforms, and sandbox statuses draw on secondary and tertiary legal sources current as of November 2026.

---

## 1. Scope, Analytical Framework, and Legal Notice

> **Regulatory Scope Notice:** This study is a comparative regulatory, statutory, and public-procurement workability assessment prepared during Phase 2 desk research. It is not formal legal advice or a legal opinion in any jurisdiction. Under Milestone 1 [Decision Gates](../milestone-1/research-design-document/decision-gates.md), each regional cluster carries a paired **Regulatory Certainty Rating** (`Clear (High Certainty)`, `Evolving (Medium Certainty)`, or `Uncertain (Low Certainty)`) and a `Specialist / Local Counsel Required` flag identifying the statutory questions that require local legal sign-off before any institutional pilot or capital deployment.

Each candidate cluster is assessed across seven statutory and administrative dimensions:

1. **Competent Authorities and Enacted Statutes:** Central banks, securities commissions, financial intelligence units, data-protection authorities, and public-procurement bodies, together with laws and circulars in force as of November 2026.
2. **Licensing, Registration, and Sandbox Regimes:** Active statutory licensing frameworks, capital thresholds, regulatory incubation cohorts, and expired or archived legislative bills.
3. **Stablecoin and Virtual-Asset Payment Posture:** Classification as `Permitted`, `Regulated`, `Restricted`, `Prohibited`, or `Unclear` across retail payments and B2B institutional settlement.
4. **Data Protection, Privacy, and Public-Ledger Rules:** Interaction between immutable on-chain state and statutory data-subject rights, including rectification, erasure, suppression, cross-border transfers, and domestic server-localisation rules.
5. **Public Procurement Rules, Contracting Thresholds, and Title Constraints:** Statutory tender requirements, direct minor-contract ceilings (Contratos Menores and low-value innovation thresholds), local shareholding and content rules, and executive title gates.
6. **Foreign Exchange (FX) Controls and Capital Rules:** Central bank FX matching rules, parallel-market spreads, Travel Rule thresholds, and cross-border settlement perimeters.
7. **Paired Regulatory Certainty Rating and Local Counsel Verification:** Distinction between statutory clarity (how clearly rules are codified) and commercial workability (the capital and compliance cost required to operate within them).

## 3. Regional Regulatory Narratives

The regional summaries below unpack the matrix entries into operational boundaries for pilot design across the three advancing hubs, the European reference benchmark, and the four non-advancing clusters.

### 3.1 Southern Cone (`Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay` — `Buenos Aires Hub`)

Argentina's legal framework combines private contract freedom with a firm separation between registered virtual-asset providers and commercial banks. Under Executive Decree DNU 70/2023 (Arts. 765-766 CCyC), private parties may denominate and settle commercial obligations in any foreign currency or virtual asset [`REF-32`], providing a statutory basis for B2B cooperative invoice and escrow settlement (`H6`, `H7`). On the supervisory side, Law 27.739 (March 2024) and CNV General Resolution 1172/2026 (published in the Official Gazette on 15 October 2026, replacing CNV GR 1058/2025 and Chapter III of Title XIV in full) [`REF-31`] govern more than 100 registered PSAVs. RG 1172/2026 retains the five activity categories, USD 35,000-150,000 minimum net-worth thresholds, custody segregation, cybersecurity audits, and UIF Resolution 49/2024 Travel Rule reporting [`REF-34`]. It also adds mandatory monthly reporting (effective November 2026) on client metrics, USD trade volumes, top traded assets, and custody balances, alongside a ban on crypto mixers [`REF-31`]. At the banking perimeter, BCRA Communications "A" 7506 and "A" 7759 — neither repealed nor superseded in BCRA's consolidated *Texto Ordenado* through November 2026 [`REF-33`] — continue to bar commercial banks and retail PSPs from balance-sheet crypto custody. Any Cardano stablecoin pilot in Argentina must therefore integrate with a CNV-registered PSAV (Lemon, Belo, Ripio, Fiwind) connected to Transferencias 3.0 through licensed bank APIs (BIND), rather than pitching direct balance-sheet custody to a bank.

Across the Rio de la Plata, Uruguay enacted Law 20.345 (September 2024) and BCU Circular 2507, placing virtual-asset providers under BCU/SSF supervision with authorisation windows extending through mid-2027 [`REF-39`]. Free FX convertibility and EU GDPR-adequate data protection (Uruguay Law 18.331 and Argentina Law 25.326) [`REF-35`, `REF-39`] make Uruguay a compatible bi-national counterparty for Mercosur cross-border pilots. Paraguay complements the cluster through SEPRELAD Resolution 313/2021 AML registration and 24/7 SIPAP interbank settlement [`REF-40`].

### 3.2 East Africa (`Kenya, Rwanda, Uganda` — `Nairobi Hub`)

East Africa enacted two major virtual-asset statutes across 2025-2026. In **Kenya**, the Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASP) Act, 2025 (Act No. 20 of 2025) took effect on 4 November 2025 under joint CBK and CMA licensing [`REF-16`]. In parallel, the Finance Act 2025 repealed the 3% gross Digital Asset Tax and replaced it with a 10% excise duty levied solely on VASP platform service fees (effective 1 July 2025) [`REF-17`]. In **Rwanda**, Parliament enacted Law No. 023/2026 of 25/05/2026 Regulating Virtual Asset Business (Official Gazette 28 May 2026, effective immediately under Art. 40) [`REF-23`]. Law 023/2026 designates CMA Rwanda as lead licensing authority with BNR payment and systemic oversight (Arts. 5-8), requires local corporate incorporation (Art. 10), and regulates 100%-collateralised stablecoin issuance (Art. 19). It also prohibits direct use of virtual assets as a means of payment without BNR authorisation (Art. 15(3)), excludes algorithmic stablecoins and anonymity-enhanced VAs (Art. 4(3)(h)-(i)), and bars tokenising PII, sovereign assets, or public infrastructure (Art. 17(1)) [`REF-23`]. Uganda allows bounded testing inside the BoU National Payment Systems sandbox [`REF-24`].

For credential and agricultural traceability deployments in Kenya (`H4`, `H5`), three legal parameters govern pilot design: (1) Under Kenya's Data Protection Act 2019, Section 40 governs rectification and erasure, Sections 48-49 govern general commercial cross-border transfers, and Section 50 read with Regulation 26 of the Data Protection (General) Regulations 2021 requires processing in Kenya or storing at least one serving copy in a Kenyan data centre for six strategic state sectors (civil registration and legal ID, elections, public finance, protected computer systems, basic education, and primary or secondary healthcare) [`REF-18`]. Hosting off-chain farmer and citizen PII on Kenyan servers (Identus agent or cooperative ERP) while anchoring only salted batch hashes on Cardano is structured to address both ss. 48-49 and s. 50 / Reg. 26, subject to local Kenyan legal counsel sign-off under `LC-07`. (2) Under EUDR (Reg. (EU) 2023/1115 as amended by Reg. (EU) 2025/2650, 19 Dec 2025), binding application starts on 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators (30 June 2027 for micro and small enterprises) [`REF-22`]. (3) Because PPADA 2015 imposes >51% citizen shareholding preference (Section 155) and 40% local-content sourcing and 30% AGPO quotas (Section 157 / Regs) on public tenders [`REF-19`], Phase 3 focuses on private export cooperatives and NGOs contracting outside state tender cycles.

### 3.3 West Africa (`Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal` — `Lagos Hub`)

Nigeria and Ghana moved from regulatory restriction to codified VASP regimes between late 2023 and early 2026. In Nigeria, CBN Circular FPR/DIR/PUB/CIR/002/003 (22 Dec 2023) lifted the 2021 banking ban on VASP settlement accounts while keeping direct bank crypto dealing prohibited [`REF-03`]. In March 2025, Nigeria enacted the Investments and Securities Act (ISA) 2025, bringing virtual and digital assets under SEC Nigeria statutory jurisdiction alongside the ARIP onboarding cohort (Busha, Quidax) [`REF-01`, `REF-02`]. On 16 January 2026, SEC Nigeria issued Circular No. 26-1 raising minimum paid-up capital for VASPs (compliance deadline 30 June 2027): NGN 2.0 billion for Digital Asset Exchanges (DAX) and Custodians, NGN 1.0 billion for DAOPs and RATOPs, NGN 500 million for Digital Asset Intermediaries (DAI) and Platform Operators (DAPO), and NGN 300 million for Ancillary VASPs (AVASPs) [`REF-01`]. A Nigerian settlement pilot (`H1`) must therefore work with well-capitalised SEC-regulated VASPs rather than small uncapitalised startups, while adhering to CBN TED/FEM/PUB/FPC/001/003 (Naira-only retail IMTO payouts) and CBN EFEMS FX rules [`REF-04`]. In Ghana, Parliament enacted the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act, 2025 (Act 1154) in December 2025, replacing the BoG sandbox with dual BoG and SEC Ghana licensing [`REF-12`]. Senegal remains inside the restrictive BCEAO / AMF-UMOA (formerly CREPMF) banking union [`REF-15`], confining Phase 3 West African pilot design to the Nigeria-Ghana corridor.

For public-sector and credential plays in Nigeria (`H2`, `H3`), three non-financial statutes set the legal perimeter: (1) NDPA 2023 (Sec. 24 processing principles, Sec. 38 portability, Secs. 24-38 rights, Secs. 41 & 43 cross-border transfer controls) requires keeping NIMC NIN and student or member PII off-chain [`REF-06`]; (2) NITDA IT Projects Clearance Guidelines require prior clearance before federal ministries execute IT procurement under the Public Procurement Act 2007 [`REF-05`, `REF-08`]; and (3) Land Use Act 1978 Section 22 mandates discretionary Governor's Consent for every statutory land alienation, blocking external public-ledger title replacement [`REF-07`].

### 3.4 Iberia (`Spain - Barcelona`, Comparative EU Benchmark)

Spain operates under codified EU statutes. Spain's shortened MiCA transitional window closed on 30 December 2025, requiring CASPs to hold CNMV or passported EU authorisation and restricting regulated stablecoin offerings to EU-authorised EMTs (Circle EURC/USDC) [`REF-49`]. Both hypotheses evaluated in the Barcelona desk benchmark (`H11` and `H12`) are non-financial, non-custodial credential and audit-log workflows that sit outside MiCA token issuance. They are governed instead by three instruments: (1) EU eIDAS 2.0 (Reg. (EU) 2024/1183), recognising EAAs and EUDI Wallets [`REF-50`]; (2) Spain Real Decreto-ley 14/2019, restricting public-administration DLT citizen ID [`REF-51`], read alongside EDPB Guidelines 02/2025 (final version 2.0 adopted 7 July 2026), which confirm that linkable public keys and hashed personal identifiers on immutable ledgers trigger GDPR obligations and require zero on-chain PII [`REF-52`]; and (3) LCSP Ley 9/2017 Art. 118 (Contratos Menores), allowing direct municipal and university contracts below EUR 15,000 (excl. VAT) without an open EU tender [`REF-53`].

### 3.5 Non-Advancing Clusters (`Brazil, Andean, Mexico, Horn / Ethiopia`)

- **Brazil (`Monitor` — 3.00 / 5.00, RARF `D4` = 3):** Issued 10 November 2025 and effective 2 February 2026, BCB Resolutions 519, 520, and 521/2025 paired with Joint CMN/BCB Resolution No. 14 establish clear statutory rules under Law 14.478/2022 alongside high entry barriers [`REF-41`]. Res. 519 governs licensing, Res. 520 governs SPSAV operations and custody, Res. 521/2025 brings cross-border stablecoin transfers under Foreign Exchange (Câmbio) rules with a USD 100,000 cap on non-FX-authorised counterparties and mandatory self-hosted wallet tracing, and Joint Res. 14 sets BRL 10.8 million to BRL 37.2 million minimum paid-in capital and net equity floors [`REF-41`]. Paired with BCB Pix (187.8M+ users) [`REF-42`] and zero local field hub, Brazil stays on `Monitor`.
- **Andean (Colombia, Peru — `Monitor` — 2.60 / 5.00, RARF `D4` = 3):** Colombia remains in a statutory VASP licensing vacuum after Draft PSAV Bill No. 510/2025 was archived in the Senate following LaArenera's expiry [`REF-46`]. Peru's SBS Resolution 02648-2024 establishes an AML and Travel Rule registry with UIF-Peru without prudential payment licensing, while Law No. 32069 (Supreme Decree 001-2026-EF, Jan 2026) restructured state procurement under OECE and PLADICOP [`REF-48`].
- **Mexico (`Monitor` — 2.55 / 5.00, RARF `D4` = 2):** Banxico Circular 4/2019 continues to bar ITFs and banks from client-facing virtual-asset services, forcing bifurcated IFPE and LFPIORPI Art. 17 XVI corporate structures (Bitso). Separately, the 2025 LFPDPPP (effective 21 March 2025) extinguished INAI and transferred private-sector privacy enforcement to SABG [`REF-44`, `REF-45`].
- **Horn / Ethiopia (`No-Go` — 2.00 / 5.00, RARF `D4` = 2):** The NBE May 2022 crypto ban and Proclamation 1282/2023 prohibit cryptocurrency settlement [`REF-26`], Directive FXD/01/2024 confines formal FX and remittances to licensed banks, Telebirr, and EthSwitch [`REF-27`, `REF-30`], and Proclamation 1284/2023 (Fayda MOSIP), Proclamation 1321/2024, INSA Proclamation 808/2013, and Procurement Proclamation 1333/2024 enforce sovereign state control [`REF-28`, `REF-29`].
