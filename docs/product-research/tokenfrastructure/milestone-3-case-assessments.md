# Cardano and comparison case assessments

These short assessments show how the twelve Cardano or Cardano-adjacent cases and eight comparison cases informed the Milestone 3 recommendation. They apply the evidence cut of 19 October 2026 and distinguish live capability from commercial adoption.

## Cardano and Cardano-adjacent cases

| Case | Short assessment | Decision consequence | Evidence references |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masumi | Live Cardano implementation of agent registration, conditional payment, refunds and service records. Observable activity establishes capability, but public evidence does not establish independent paying buyers, repeat use or accepted outcomes. | Strongest Cardano proof point and reference beachhead; conditional rather than proven commercial adoption. | S28-S31, S50, S53-S57, S68-S69, S73 |
| Sokosumi | Live commercial surface with priced agent services and a task workflow built around Masumi. The provider-reported task count does not separate paid work, free activity, repeat buyers, refunds or outcome quality. | Useful vertical example; expansion should depend on buyer and transaction-quality evidence. | S30, S58-S60, S70 |
| Cardano x402 scheme | Official material documents direct, script and Masumi escrow payment methods. Public evidence does not show comparable facilitator, wallet, stablecoin or seller distribution. | Interoperate with existing payment standards; do not claim machine-payment leadership. | S25, S49-S50 |
| Iagon | Observable Cardano activity supports a live storage or compute capability, but no strong public buyer, workload, revenue or Cardano-specific AI demand evidence was identified. | Do not use Iagon to justify a broad Cardano compute strategy without independent demand evidence. | S26-S27, S32 |
| SingularityNET | The project has a longstanding Cardano association, but current tracked Cardano activity and buyer evidence are limited. | Historical affiliation is not sufficient evidence of a current Cardano AI adoption vertical. | S26-S27 |
| NuNet | NuNet documents a live decentralized-compute network and prior Cardano pilots, but its current production dependency on Cardano is weak or unproven. | Treat as an adjacent or comparison case, not direct Cardano adoption. | S39 |
| Rejuve.AI | The roadmap supports Cardano wallets, while relevant digital-asset contracts also require an EVM-compatible wallet. Current Cardano-dependent usage is not established. | Do not count it as Cardano-native adoption without current production evidence. | S38 |
| Veridian | Credible identity infrastructure based on KERI and ACDC with Cardano Foundation origins. Public architecture does not establish a requirement for Cardano settlement. | Potential identity partner or supporting capability, not a unique Cardano chain advantage. | S16-S17, S36 |
| Midnight | Strong privacy and selective-disclosure capability in the wider ecosystem, but it is a separate network rather than a native Cardano L1 property. | Handoff to the privacy workstream; avoid inheriting Midnight claims for Cardano L1. | S37 |
| Hydra | Mainnet-compatible state-channel technology exists, with documented operating constraints and development risk. No relevant production agent-payment reference was identified. | Do not base performance claims or funding on Hydra without a named production reference. | S35 |
| UVerify and notary applications | Current activity shows that tamper-evident anchoring can operate on Cardano. Public evidence does not establish AI-specific buyer demand or the need for a public ledger. | Supporting evidence for receipt anchoring only; buyer and non-chain alternative tests remain necessary. | S26-S27 |
| Bending AI and Hizz | Directory listings show ecosystem experimentation, but no adequate usage, buyer, revenue or chain-necessity evidence was identified. | Do not treat listings as strategic AI adoption evidence. | S26 |

## Comparison cases

| Comparison case | Short assessment | Decision consequence | Evidence references |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masumi and Sokosumi on Cardano | The combined protocol and marketplace provide the clearest live example of conditional agent-service settlement on Cardano. Commercial depth remains provisional. | Use as the reference starting point, not proof of a broad AI vertical. | S25, S28-S31, S50, S53-S60, S68-S70, S73 |
| x402 on Solana and Base | Stronger visible facilitator, developer and ecosystem distribution than Cardano. Activity metrics still do not by themselves prove durable enterprise demand or transaction quality. | Cardano needs a differentiated escrow or assurance outcome and a credible integration route; generic payments are insufficient. | S05-S07, S40, S43, S71 |
| MPP and Tempo | Open, payment-focused infrastructure supports multiple rails and efficient payment sessions. Independent scale and long-term economics remain early. | Compete after payment initiation through conditional release and evidence, not on payment transport alone. | S08-S11, S41 |
| UCP, ACP and AP2 | Existing commerce infrastructure can support agent discovery, mandates and checkout while preserving merchant control and familiar payment operations. | Mainstream consumer checkout is a no-go as a broad Cardano position; consider only defined exceptions. | S03-S04, S12-S15 |
| Visa and Mastercard agent initiatives | Established networks bring merchant reach, fraud controls, permissions, reconciliation and liability frameworks. Production depth is still developing, but distribution is materially stronger. | Position Cardano as a selective complement for cross-organization settlement or evidence, not a replacement. | S12-S14 |
| ERC-8004 and ERC-8183 | Draft Ethereum standards cover agent identity, reputation, validation and escrowed job completion, showing that the proposed primitives are not unique to Cardano. | Differentiate through a working product, integration and buyer outcomes rather than a feature list. | S44-S45 |
| Bittensor, Akash and NuNet | Specialist AI and compute networks have clearer supply-side positioning, developer focus and market structure than current Cardano cases. | Compute and model markets remain no-go areas unless a buyer identifies a Cardano-specific need. | S39, S46-S48 |
| W3C VC, OpenID4VP, C2PA and Veridian | Mature identity, credential and provenance approaches can solve many trust and evidence problems without public-chain settlement. | Require a specific neutrality, ordering or settlement benefit; otherwise use interoperable or hybrid alternatives. | S16-S18, S36, S42, S72 |

## Read-through for the recommendation

The case set supports a narrow conditional-go position around escrowed agent services and minimal shared receipts. It supports no-go calls for a broad Cardano AI narrative, direct competition in compute or model markets, mainstream consumer checkout and claims of machine-payment distribution leadership. Identity, privacy and high-frequency settlement remain supporting or adjacent capabilities unless buyer evidence shows that they change a real purchase, risk or dispute outcome.
