# Use-case matrix

Weights: demand 20%; chain need 15%; Cardano fit 20%; evidence 15%; buyer access 10%; differentiation 10%; feasibility 10%. Scores use a 1–5 scale.

| ID | Use case | Demand | Chain need | Cardano fit | Evidence | Buyer access | Differentiation | Feasibility | Score | Posture | Reason | Funding trigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| U01 | Escrowed agent-service purchase | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3.65 | Priority conditional go | Live Masumi capability and a clear non-delivery problem; independent paid demand remains unproven. | Two independent service providers and three external buyers define a paid workflow and acceptance criteria. |
| U02 | Pay-per-call API and data access with conditional protection | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3.55 | Priority validation | Large protocol activity exists, but plain x402 or MPP is simpler unless refund or service risk matters. | A buyer demonstrates willingness to choose conditional settlement over an existing facilitator route. |
| U04 | Tamper-evident agent action and payment receipts | 4 | 3 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3.50 | Conditional test | Strong technical fit for cross-organization evidence; legal and operational value requires buyer validation. | Two risk or audit owners accept a minimal receipt schema and privacy design. |
| U05 | AI-agent marketplace on Masumi and Sokosumi | 3 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3.25 | Conditional test | A working market surface exists, but paid conversion, concentration, retention and outcome quality are unclear. | Provider reports separate paid, completed, refunded, disputed and repeat transactions with independent buyer confirmation. |
| U03 | Payment-linked agent identity and reputation | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3.20 | Conditional test | Useful as part of service assurance, but mature credential standards and competing registries limit uniqueness. | A cross-platform buyer decision demonstrably changes because of portable payment-linked history. |
| U06 | Enterprise mandate enforcement for autonomous spend | 4 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 2.85 | Monitor and test | The control need is real, but AP2, AuthZEN, cards and wallet controls have stronger current distribution. | A multi-party workflow requires neutral enforcement that existing policy and payment systems cannot provide. |
| U07 | Content and data provenance | 4 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 2.80 | Monitor | Demand exists, but C2PA and signed registries are strong non-chain alternatives. | A buyer requires shared ordering or transferable rights beyond signed manifests. |
| U09 | AI-enabled DeFi automation | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2.35 | Monitor | Blockchain need is clearer, but risk, competition and Cardano-specific demand remain weak. | A regulated buyer defines an autonomous workflow with enforceable limits and accepted liability. |
| U08 | Privacy-preserving AI transactions | 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 2.30 | Adjacent handoff | Midnight is relevant but separate; the Cardano integration and buyer need are not proven. | A joint architecture proves privacy, interoperability, settlement and operational viability. |
| U10 | Decentralized compute marketplace | 5 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2.25 | No-go | Specialized networks and clouds have stronger supply, product focus and distribution. | Reopen only on evidence of a Cardano-specific buyer or compliance requirement. |
| U12 | Generic AI analytics or assistant applications | 4 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 4 | 2.15 | Exclude from vertical proof | AI is a feature and does not establish a blockchain-dependent market. | None within this positioning study. |
| U11 | Decentralized model or inference market | 4 | 4 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2.05 | No-go | Bittensor and specialized AI networks are structurally better positioned. | Reopen only if a distinct Cardano buyer requirement emerges. |
