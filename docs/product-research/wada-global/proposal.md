## 1. Executive Summary
Wada Global submits this multi-hub research proposal to the Cardano

Product Committee (CPC) targeting RFP 04: Brand Awareness and

Perception. This initiative shifts the focus from a single-market study to a

strategic cross-border assessment that leverages the established, active

regional coordinator networks of Wada Global in Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana.

These are the primary pillars of Cardano visibility and grass-roots activity

in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Cardano enjoys localized visibility in these tech capitals but scaling to

institutional enterprise deployments, cross-border fintech integrations and

mainstream developer migration requires hard and comparative data. This

project will establish a uniform market perception baseline across all three

nations and evaluate how external developers, capital allocators and

enterprise buyers position Cardano against aggressive regional

competitors like Solana and Ethereum Layer 2 frameworks.

This research operates as an active message-testing pilot rather than

conducting passive academic observation. We will actively stress-test

current Cardano positioning to document real-world resistance, friction

points and onboarding objections in real time by treating external regional

software hubs and enterprise buyers as a live target segment.

## 2. Understanding of the Brief
RFP 04 asks what current perception, competitive landscape narratives and

strategic recommendations are required to convert external developer and

enterprise interest into ecosystem growth. The brief emphasizes that

expansion requires deep and unbiased external evidence regarding how

the broader Web3 ecosystem evaluates Cardano's technology stack.

Wada understands the brief as a call for practical evidence, not theory. The

research would therefore show who the external developers and enterprise

buyers are, what specific technological barriers block their entry, what core

misconceptions drive competitor narrative advantages and what

communication adjustments can the ecosystem make to secure regional

product-market fit. This proposal narrows the RFP 04 question to an active

2

multi-hub testing approach across Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana in a manner

that defines how Cardano's core value propositions land when actively

presented to decision-makers who do not currently use the network. It is a

rigorous research and pilot-design proposal to establish an

uncompromised market perception baseline.

## 3. Problem Statement
Expanding Cardano's regional footprint into institutional partnerships and

enterprise deployment requires deep and unbiased external evidence. The

ecosystem feedback loops are often locked within internal community

channels in recent times which leads to a high risk of groupthink. When

moving outside established community channels, the transition to external

developer and enterprise adoption faces significant friction. The exact

reasons why regional software hubs choose specific competing

frameworks remain undocumented.

Key problems include:

● No clear regional brand perception baseline across major African

tech capitals.

● Limited visibility into external software developer entry barriers

regarding Plutus and Aiken.

● Fragmented understanding of why local fintech buyers prefer Solidity

or Rust environments.

● Unclear visibility into how local regulatory frameworks impact

institutional trust in the network.

● Weak multi-hub feedback mechanisms to route localized ecosystem

friction to proper technical channels.

● Inconsistent tracking of competitor positioning advantages (such as

Solana or Ethereum L2s).

● Macro blockchain invest ability narratives built on incomplete or

outdated ecosystem data.

## 4. Core Research Question
What uniform market perception baseline and competitive positioning

framework will help the Cardano Product Committee decode external

3

narratives, classify local technical objections and optimize go-to-market

strategies across the key technological hubs of Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana?

## 5. Project Objectives
Wada Global will:

1. Map the current external brand perception of Cardano across three

strategic African tech hubs.

2. Identify why external software developers choose peer ecosystems

over Cardano.

3. Define the specific technical entry barriers associated with Plutus

and Aiken adoption.

4. Develop a regional Objection Register to classify localized marketing

and liquidity gaps.

5. Assess how external enterprise, fintech, and institutional buyers

evaluate Cardano's regulatory trust.

6. Identify what public narrative structures and invest-ability shorthand

drive local capital allocation.

7. Recommend an actionable, data-backed playbook for regional

message positioning adjustments.

8. Synthesize findings into officially formatted, GitHub-ready Cardano

Problem Statements (CPS).

## 6. Proposed Methodology
This project utilizes a synchronized mixed-method approach executed

simultaneously across all three target countries. Strict screening rules

ensure that current Cardano community members are filtered out of the

primary external data pools.

### 6.1 Multi-Hub Pilot Lens
The research will target three specific regional coordinator networks:

● Wada Nigeria: Understand high-volume fintech perceptions, capital

allocation shorthand and large-scale developer narratives.

4

● Wada Kenya: Understand micro-finance integration parameters, local

regulatory sandboxes and mobile-first tech barriers.

● Wada Ghana: Understand educational onboarding pipelines,

institutional trust baselines and local tech hub entry criteria.

### 6.2 Desk Research
Wada will review regional Web3 adoption patterns in Sub-Saharan Africa,

competitor ecosystem marketing strategies, regional fintech regulatory

documentation and past Cardano ecosystem data points to isolate the

gap between internal beliefs and external reality.

### 6.3 Stakeholder Interviews
Wada will conduct 30 highly targeted qualitative key-informant interviews

across the three sponsored hubs (10 interviews per hub):

● External Developers and Builders (Unfamiliar with Cardano): 9 total

● Enterprise, Fintech and Corporate Buyers: 12 total

● Capital Allocators, Analysts and Tech Media Voices: 9 total

### 6.4 Active Message-Testing Sandbox
Sponsored ground teams will actively present external target segments

with core Cardano value propositions (such as Midnight privacy layers,

Aiken smart contract efficiency and RealFI liquidity models) to stress-test

market perception and record real-world technical objections in real time.

secure voluntary respondent participation through direct outreach via

established networks in Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana coordinator nodes. We

will engage domestic fintech groups, regional developer networks,

university partnerships and local enterprise circles. During kick-off, Wada

central management will coordinate with regional hub leads to classify

access levels as confirmed, likely, or requiring support.

## 11. Brand Perception / Readiness Framework
Because this is an applied research initiative, Wada will not offer formal

certification. Instead, the project will design a structured Brand Perception

and Readiness Framework to score target market segments into

classifications: *Ecosystem Adversarial, Aware but Obstructed, Stack *

*Agnostic,* and *Ecosystem Cultivated*. Evidence collected during hub

validation will include current programming language preferences,

perceived network speed rankings and familiarity with Cardano-native

enterprise tooling.

## 12. Cardano Relevance Assessment
Wada will evaluate where Cardano's current architectural features serve as

a brand advantage and where technical changes are required to capture

the market. This includes positioning Midnight's data privacy layers against

regional corporate compliance expectations ostensibly to leverage Aiken's

smart contract efficiency and utilizing Identus/Atala PRISM verifiable

credentials. The research will honestly assess where Cardano features

present current adoption friction to ensure objective feedback rather than

internal ecosystem hype.

## 13. Shared-Value Ecosystem Model
Wada Global will analyse how future go-to-market and messaging

playbooks can use shared regional structures to maximize long-term

adoption value. The study will test whether regional tech incubators would

co-sponsor localized Cardano developer onboarding tracks if educational

resources are simplified and what minimum infrastructure setup or

documentation baseline local systems integrators require before

presenting Cardano options to their enterprise clients.

## 14. Systems Integrator & Enterprise Barriers
This research focuses heavily on identifying what prevents local systems

integrators, enterprise technology firms and local consulting layers from

8

building on Cardano. The multi-hub study will document the technical,

legal or commercial hurdles that stop regional software agencies from

offering Cardano-native engineering services and determine what specific

training material or code libraries would make network selection easier for

local engineering leads.

## 15. Geography and Sector Gap Approach
The geographic focus spans the key technology capitals of Nigeria, Kenya

and Ghana, comparing software engineering, corporate finance, enterprise

logistics, and regional capital allocation sectors. The cross-border analysis

will identify regional variations in developer entry barriers, localized

regulatory environments, variations in mobile-first infrastructure readiness

and distinct narrative shorthand driving investor perception across Lagos,

Nairobi, and Accra.

## 16. Training and Enablement Assessment
Wada Global will define exact training, documentation and tooling

enablement needs required to shift external brand perception:

● External Software Engineers: Tooling orientation, language translation

pathways (moving from Solidity to Aiken), and localized code

registries.

● Enterprise Decision Makers: Plain-English capability briefs,

compliance-matching guides and verifiable data security case

studies.

● Regional Fund Managers: Risk-mitigation documentation, commercial

viability models, and global network performance tracking data.

## 17. Governance and Quality-Control Approach
Wada will use strict data governance and control parameters to ensure the

absolute integrity of this research project. Controls include multi-stage

respondent filtering to completely exclude existing Cardano community

bias from external data pools, anonymization protocols to protect the

commercial privacy of participating enterprises and standardized

9

formatting for all compiled Objection Registers and Cardano Problem

Statements to allow seamless programmatic evaluation by Intersect.

## 18. Pilot Pathway Approach
Wada Global will recommend a structured, regional framework for future

message-testing pilots based on the collected dataset. The proposed

evaluation loop involves target external segment identification, recording

baseline perception metrics, actively presenting localized value

propositions via the testing sandbox, logging technical objections in real

time and adjusting messaging based on regional playbook

recommendations to measure conversion rates.

## 19. Data Sources
Wada will combine diverse data layers to build an uncompromised

baseline: synchronized quantitative digital surveys across the three

regional hub networks, 30 qualitative key-informant interviews with verified

external industry leaders, cross-border competitive positioning data,

domestic policy documentation and on-the-ground operational field logs

compiled by sponsored ground teams.

## 20. Workplan
● Package 1 (Weeks 1–2): Project setup, screener finalization and cross-

hub data alignment.

● Package 2 (Weeks 3–6): Localized hub fieldwork deployment, external

surveys and 30 qualitative interviews across all three nations.

● Package 3 (Weeks 7–10): Cross-border benchmarking, narrative gap

calculations, compilation of the master Objection Register and

formatting the GitHub-ready CPS document.

## 24. Risk and Bias Mitigation Plan
● Internal Echo Chamber / Community Bias: Implement strict screening

controls to completely exclude current Cardano community

members from primary survey pools.

● Data Overgeneralization: Distinctly isolate and label data findings by

country (Nigeria vs. Kenya vs. Ghana) and target segment.

11

● Cardano Overfitting: Ensure objective data tracking; clearly

document where conventional infrastructure or competitor systems

outshine Cardano.

● Confidentiality / Corporate Risk: Completely anonymize all sensitive

enterprise, corporate and investor respondent data to protect market

standing.

## 25. Confidentiality and Data Handling
Wada Global will enforce rigorous security and data collection protocols to

obtain explicit informed consent prior to all stakeholder interviews. This will

fully anonymize all participating corporate entities and restrict access to

raw multi-hub field data to the core project team via secure data

repositories.

## 26. Ethics Approach
This research project adheres to strict professional ethics guidelines for

purposes of avoiding interference with ongoing commercial tech stack

deployments. This is sure to protect the operational privacy of participating

businesses to avoid negative branding regarding competitor frameworks in

addition to explicitly distinguishing raw participant data from strategic

interpretations.

# 1. Executive Summary

Wada Global submits this multi-hub research proposal to the Cardano Product Committee (CPC) targeting RFP 04: Brand Awareness and Perception. This initiative shifts the focus from a single-market study to a strategic cross-border assessment that leverages the established, active regional coordinator networks of Wada Global in Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana. These are the primary pillars of Cardano visibility and grass-roots activity in Sub-Saharan Africa.

Cardano enjoys localized visibility in these tech capitals but scaling to institutional enterprise deployments, cross-border fintech integrations and mainstream developer migration requires hard and comparative data. This project will establish a uniform market perception baseline across all three nations and evaluate how external developers, capital allocators and enterprise buyers position Cardano against aggressive regional competitors like Solana and Ethereum Layer 2 frameworks.

This research operates as an active message-testing pilot rather than conducting passive academic observation. We will actively stress-test current Cardano positioning to document real-world resistance, friction points and onboarding objections in real time by treating external regional software hubs and enterprise buyers as a live target segment.

## 2. Understanding the Brief

RFP 04 asks what current perception, competitive landscape narratives and strategic recommendations are required to convert external developer and enterprise interest into ecosystem growth. The brief emphasizes that expansion requires deep and unbiased external evidence regarding how the broader Web3 ecosystem evaluates Cardano's technology stack.

Wada understands the brief as a call for practical evidence, not theory. The research would therefore show how the external developers and enterprise buyers are, what specific technological barriers block their entry, what core misconceptions drive competitor narrative advantages and what communication adjustments can the ecosystem make to secure regional product-market fit. This proposal narrows the RFP 04 question to an active


multi-hub testing approach across Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana in a manner that defines how Cardano's core value propositions land when actively presented to decision-makers who do not currently use the network. It is a rigorous research and pilot-design proposal to establish an uncompromlyised market perception baseline.

## 3. Problem Statement

Expanding Cardano's regional footprint into institutional partnerships and enterprise deployment requires deep and unbiased external evidence. The ecosystem feedback loops are often locked within internal community channels in recent times which leads to a high risk of groupthink. When moving outside established community channels, the transition to external developer and enterprise adoption faces significant friction. The exact reasons why regional software hubs choose specific competing frameworks remain undocumented.

Key problems include:

- No clear regional brand perception baseline across major African tech capitals.
- Limited visibility into external software developer entry barriers regarding Plutus and Aiken.
- Fragmented understanding of why local fintech buyers prefer Solidity or Rust environments.
- Unclear visibility into how local regulatory frameworks impact institutional trust in the network.
- Weak multi-hub feedback mechanisms to route localized ecosystem friction to proper technical channels.
- Inconsistent tracking of competitor positioning advantages (such as Solana or Ethereum L2s).
- Macro blockchain investability narratives built on incomplete or outdated ecosystem data.

## 4. Core Research Question

What uniform market perception baseline and competitive positioning framework will help the Cardano Product Committee decode external


narratives, classify local technical objections and optimize go-to-market strategies across the key technological hubs of Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana?
