# **1\. Purpose and Objectives** {#1.-purpose-and-objectives}

This document sets out the research design for Milestone 1 of the Multi-Hub Message-Testing Baseline project. It is delivered by Wada Global for the Cardano Product Committee through IntersectMBO. The purpose of this milestone is to establish one shared method for measuring how developers, enterprise buyers and capital allocators in Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana currently perceive Cardano and to make sure the three hubs collect data the same way so that the results can be compared fairly across countries.

The research answers the question set out in the Grant Agreement in Appendix A. That question reflects what current brand perception exists across the three hubs, why external developers choose other ecosystems over Cardano, what barriers stop enterprise buyers from trusting Cardano and how Cardano compares with Solana and Ethereum Layer 2 networks.

The specific objectives for this milestone are as follows.

* Set out the full research design and the rationale behind each method chosen (see Section 2).  
* Build one cross-hub alignment plan so that Nigeria, Kenya, and Ghana run the same study rather than three separate ones (see Section 3).  
* Draft the survey instrument used for the quantitative baseline (see Section 4).  
* Draft the interview guide used for the qualitative key informant interviews (see Section 5).  
* Set the sampling framework that defines who counts as a valid respondent, how many respondents are needed and how they will be reached (see Section 6).

# **2\. Methodological Approach and Rationale** {#2.-methodological-approach-and-rationale}

This project uses a convergent mixed methods design. It means that the quantitative survey and the qualitative interviews are collected in the same window and then merged for interpretation, rather than one method leading and the other following behind it. Creswell and Creswell (2023) describe this convergent structure as the right fit when a single method cannot answer the research question fully on its own. A survey can tell us how widely a view is held, expressed as a proportion or a mean score but it cannot explain the reasoning sitting underneath that number. An interview on the other hand can surface that reasoning in detail but it cannot tell us how common the view actually is on its own. Running both at once and then checking where they agree and where they pull apart gives a sturdier answer than either method could give alone.

The survey treats awareness of Cardano and willingness to act on that awareness as two separate constructs rather than folding them into one combined score. This distinction has direct support in recent blockchain adoption research. Ennajeh and Najjar (2024) applied the Unified Theory of Acceptance and Use of Technology to blockchain and specifically found that trust in the technology acts as a mediating variable  that sits between performance expectancy and actual adoption intention. In plain terms, a respondent can rate Cardano highly on capability and still decline to build on it, invest in it or recommend it if trust has not been separately earned. This is the reasoning behind keeping awareness and consideration as distinct measures in the survey. It further clarifies the idea behind asking respondents directly what proof would change their mind and not just assuming that knowing about a feature automatically converts into willingness to use it.

The interviews exist to explain the reasoning behind the survey numbers. Thirty key informant interviews are planned across the three hubs. They are split across external developers, enterprise and fintech buyers and capital allocators and analysts. Pahwa, Cavanagh and Vanstone (2023) argue that key informant interviewing works best as a purposive criterion based sampling strategy where people are selected because they hold a specific and relevant vantage point on the question at hand and not because they represent a random cross section of the population. Their guidance also flags a risk worth taking seriously here, which is that a key informant can end up speaking on behalf of a group without being accountable to it. To manage that, the interview guide is written to ask about each respondent's own direct experience and reasoning. This solves the challenge of treating any one person as a spokesperson for an entire market. Transcripts will be coded using reflexive thematic analysis, thus following the six phase process set out by Braun and Clarke (2021): familiarisation with the data, generating initial codes, constructing candidate themes, reviewing those themes against the full dataset, defining and naming them and producing the write up. This is a reflexive process that gives some credibility to the work because the analyst's judgement about what counts as a genuine barrier versus a passing preference is itself part of the finding. 

Because this project compares perception across three countries, the survey and interview instruments need to carry the same meaning in every hub. Researchers refer to this as measurement invariance or more plainly, construct equivalence. Soler-Anguiano, Rivera-Aragón and Díaz-Loving (2023) show that a correctly translated question can still end up measuring something different once it crosses into another cultural setting. This is because respondents bring different background assumptions to the same words. Full statistical invariance testing of the kind used in large academic surveys sits outside the scope of a ten week applied project, but the underlying discipline still applies here. The same instrument is used in all three hubs with only minor logged local adjustments. For instance, swapping in a locally recognised fintech product as an example where needed and every change is reviewed by all three hub leads together before fieldwork begins. This is the practical field level version of construct equivalence that keeps the comparison fair across Lagos, Nairobi and Accra.

Finally, the project treats Cardano's own community members as a source of bias rather than a valid data source. Respondents already active in the Cardano ecosystem are excluded from the primary sample because their views are not representative of the external audience the research question is actually about. This screening rule is applied consistently across all three hubs and is detailed in the sampling framework in Section 6\.

# **3\. Cross-Hub Alignment Plan** {#3.-cross-hub-alignment-plan}

The three hubs will run the same research design in parallel but not just working independently. The alignment plan has four parts.

First, a single master instrument is built centrally by the Wada Global core team. This includes the survey and the interview guide, both shown in full below.

Second, each hub lead reviews the master instrument for local fit before fieldwork starts. A hub lead may flag a question that does not translate well locally but cannot change what the question is actually measuring. Any agreed local adjustment, for example swapping a competitor example for one more familiar in that market is recorded in a shared change log so that every hub works from the same version at the same time.

Third, all three hubs apply the same screening rules to decide who qualifies as a respondent. This is covered in Section 6\.

Fourth, a joint kickoff call brings together all three hub leads before fieldwork begins, thus to walk through the instrument question by question and agree on interpretation. This is the step that reduces the risk of two interviewers in two different countries asking the same question in two different ways. This refers to a form of interviewer drift that would otherwise undermine the cross-hub comparison this project is built to produce.

# **4\. Survey Instrument** {#4.-survey-instrument}

The survey runs through a [digital form](https://forms.gle/xfsSuxkKf1AJJviv7) and takes about eight to ten minutes to complete. It is structured in five parts: screener, awareness and association, perception, competitive comparison, and consideration and objections.

Part A: Screener

**A1.** Which country are you currently based in? (Nigeria / Kenya / Ghana / Other, please specify)

**A2.** Which best describes your current role? (Software developer or builder / Enterprise or fintech decision maker / Investor, analyst or capital allocator / Other, please specify)

**A3.** Are you currently an active contributor to the Cardano ecosystem, either paid or unpaid? (Yes / No)

*Respondents answering Yes to A3 are screened out of the primary sample and logged separately as an excluded insider group.*

Part B: Awareness and Association

**B1.** Before this survey, had you heard of Cardano? (Yes / No)

**B2.** Without looking anything up, what is the first word or phrase that comes to mind when you hear of Cardano?

**B3.** Which of the following blockchain ecosystems are you familiar with? Select all that apply. (Solana / Ethereum and its Layer 2 networks / Cardano / Other, please specify)

Part C: Perceived Strengths and Weaknesses

**C1.** In your view, what is Cardano's biggest strength, if any?

**C2.** In your view, what is Cardano's biggest weakness or barrier, if any?

**C3.** Rate the following on a five point scale from strongly disagree to strongly agree.

* C3a. Cardano is easy for a new developer to start building on.  
* C3b. Cardano has strong enterprise and compliance credibility.  
* C3c. Cardano has an active and visible developer community in my country.

Part D: Competitive Comparison

**D1.** Compared with Solana, do you see Cardano as more attractive, equally attractive, or less attractive for your own use case?

**D2.** Compared with Ethereum and its Layer 2 networks, do you see Cardano as more attractive, equally attractive, or less attractive for your own use case?

**D3.** What would need to be true for you to rate Cardano more highly than the competitors you just compared it with?

Part E: Consideration and Objections

**E1.** How likely are you to consider building on, investing in, or recommending Cardano in the next twelve months? (five point scale, very unlikely to very likely)

**E2.** What is the single biggest reason stopping you from considering Cardano more seriously right now?

**E3.** What kind of proof or evidence would change your mind? For example named enterprise deployments, developer tooling improvements, case studies, security audits, or other.

# **5\. Interview Guide** {#5.-interview-guide}

The interview guide is built for a forty five minute semi structured conversation. It will be used the same way across all three hubs with follow up probes left open for the interviewer to pursue naturally rather than scripted word for word.

Opening

**1\.** Can you tell me a little about your work and how blockchain technology fits into it, if at all?

**2\.** What blockchain ecosystems, if any, do you currently work with or pay attention to?

Awareness and Perception

**3\.** What comes to mind when you think about Cardano before I say anything else about it?

**4\.** Where did that impression come from? A specific project, a conversation, something you read or something else.

Credibility and Trust

**5\.** If you were deciding whether to build on, invest in or recommend a blockchain platform, what would you need to see before you trusted Cardano specifically?

**6\.** Is there anything about Cardano that makes you hesitant even if you cannot point to a specific bad experience?

Competitive Comparison

**7\.** How does Cardano compare with Solana or Ethereum Layer 2 networks in your mind? What is different?

**8\.** Have you seen any of these ecosystems win over developers or enterprises in your market? What did they do?

Proof and Objections

**9\.** What single piece of evidence, if it existed, would change how seriously you take Cardano?

**10\.** Is there anything that has actively stopped you or someone you know from using Cardano?

Closing

**11\.** Is there anything I have not asked that you think matters for understanding how people in your market see Cardano?

# **6\. Sampling Framework** {#6.-sampling-framework}

The sampling framework applies the same rules in all three hubs.

## 6.1. Quantitative Survey {#6.1.-quantitative-survey}

The target is a minimum of ninety completed surveys per country for a combined total of at least two hundred and seventy across Nigeria, Kenya and Ghana. Respondents are recruited through hub coordinator networks, university partnerships, fintech and developer community groups and local enterprise contacts. Every respondent must pass the screener in Part A. Anyone who answers Yes to being an active paid/unpaid contributor to the Cardano ecosystem is excluded from the main sample.

## 6.2. Qualitative Interviews {#6.2.-qualitative-interviews}

Thirty interviews in total, ten per hub, split as shown in the table below. This matches the split described in the original Proposal with external developers at nine in total, enterprise and corporate buyers at twelve in total and capital allocators and analysts at nine in total.

| Hub | Survey Target | Interviews (Developers) | Interviews (Enterprise/Fintech) | Interviews (Analysts/Capital) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Nigeria | 90 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Kenya | 90 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Ghana | 90 | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Total | 270 | 9 | 12 | 9 |

## 6.3. Recruitment Quality Control {#6.3.-recruitment-quality-control}

Before an interview is logged as valid, each hub lead confirms that the respondent meets the role definition for their category and is not a current paid Cardano contributor. A short recruitment log is kept for every respondent, recording how they were found and confirming they meet the screening criteria. This creates a paper trail that Intersect can check if requested.

## 6.4. Limitations {#6.4.-limitations}

This sample is not a random probability sample of the general population and is not intended to be. It follows a purposive criterion based sampling logic, thus selecting respondents whose professional vantage point is directly relevant to Cardano's adoption decisions in these three markets (Pahwa et al., 2023). Findings should therefore be read as directional, criterion sampled evidence and not as a nationally representative statistic.
