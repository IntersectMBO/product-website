**Document Purpose**

This is the final Research Methodology Appendix (D11). It documents how the research was done so that the Committee and ecosystem readers can judge the quality of every finding. It covers the six required components: respondent categories and recruitment, instruments, evidence limits, proprietary data restrictions, the confidence rubric, and limitations including scope boundaries, access constraints and respondent bias.

Respondents who chose anonymised or confidential treatment are identified by ID and category only.

**1. Summary**

- **Design:** desk research and screening (Milestone 1), 31 interviews across four respondent categories (Milestone 2), and classification and drafting (Milestone 3), all mapped to RFP 07's eleven decision gates.
- **Evidence chain:** every finding traces from an interview or desk source, to a numbered evidence extract, to a seven-alternative Non-Layer 2 test, to a classification, to the deliverable that uses it.
- **Confidence:** High, Medium or Low on one rubric throughout, with two caps: provider-side findings are held at Low because of Category 3 access, and our own validation work cannot set any finding above Low.
- **Main limitations:** one Category 3 interview against a committed five to eight; scale is self-reported; most Cardano builders interviewed chose Cardano by familiarity rather than comparison.

**3.2 Recruitment method**

- **Access pathways** were set before outreach ([Stakeholder Access Status Update](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_StakeholderAccessStatusUpdate_v1_05Aug2026.md), Section 2): direct relationships and community networks for Category 1, a mix of direct access and structured outreach for Categories 2 and 4, and structured outreach only for Category 3, which had no prior relationships.
- **Candidate pools** were sized above target to absorb non-response: 14 candidates for Category 2 and 12 for Category 3.
- **Screening:** a respondent screener assigned each respondent to a category and, for Category 3, to the bridge and messaging variant or the wallet variant ([Interview Instruments](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_InterviewInstruments_v1_05Aug2026.md), Section 1).
- **Consent before questions:** every respondent was told the purpose, the commissioning body and how their input would be used, and chose named, anonymised or confidential treatment. Recording required separate consent. Choices were logged in the consent tracking register on the day of contact.
- **Funding-seeking flag:** respondents seeking funding were flagged at logging, so their claims could be separated from independent demand evidence.
- **Desk-research candidates** that surfaced after the design (for example African operators with documented chain selections) were carried to screening, not added directly to the respondent list.

**5. Analysis Method**

| Step | What happens | Where recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Extraction | Each distinct statement bearing on a decision gate is recorded as one evidence extract, paraphrased in the respondent's own terms, with type of statement, consent tier and the output it feeds. Interpretation is kept out of this step. | Workbook sheet 2 (500 extracts) |
| 2. Non-Layer 2 test | Every blocker claim is tested against seven alternatives: liquidity, wallet support, bridge or provider access, tooling, commercial uncertainty, compliance and partner access. | 3. **6. Evidence Limits**

- **What counts as evidence.** An adoption signal ties a capability to a decision: a deployment, migration, shutdown, build choice or payment. Roadmap existence, technical possibility, sentiment, event interest, self-attestation, market-size statistics, and bridge volume or token supply without source, destination and retention analysis do not count on their own ([Research Design Document](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_ResearchDesignDocument_v1.1_01Aug2026.md) Section 6.1).
- **Scale is self-reported.** User and transaction figures given by respondents or projects were not independently verified.
- **No flow data.** No bridge or stablecoin flow data with source, destination and retention analysis was available, so no value-flow classification relies on volume or supply.
- **Status sources.** Some current-status checks rely on secondary sources that could not be verified directly.
- **Our own validation work** is applicant-supplied conflicted evidence. It is kept separate from independent evidence and cannot set any finding above Low ([Research Design Document](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_ResearchDesignDocument_v1.1_01Aug2026.md) Section 6.4).

**7. Proprietary Data Restrictions**

- No proprietary, paid or non-public dataset was used. All primary data came from interviews, and desk research used public sources.
- Had any become necessary, its source, access conditions, inspection and citation rights, limitations and retention would have been disclosed to the Committee first ([Research Design Document](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_ResearchDesignDocument_v1.1_01Aug2026.md) Section 7.4).
- Interview notes, transcripts, recordings and the respondent identity key are held privately by the research team and retained for at least two years after project completion (Proposal Section 12), longer than the 12 months grant clause 4.3 requires. They are available to the Committee on request.

**8. Confidence Rubric**

| Level | Definition ([Research Design Document](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_ResearchDesignDocument_v1.1_01Aug2026.md) Section 6.2) |
|---|---|
| High | Supported by multiple independent evidence types with no material contradicting evidence |
| Medium | Supported by at least two evidence types, or one strong independent source, with limited contradicting evidence |
| Low | Supported by a single source, by our own validation work only, or by evidence that cannot be independently verified |

**Caps applied**

- **Provider side:** with one Category 3 interview against a committed five to eight, provider-side findings and Decision Gates 6 to 9 are held at Low ([Stakeholder Access Status Update](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_StakeholderAccessStatusUpdate_v1_05Aug2026.md), Section 4.2).
- **Own validation work:** cannot set a finding above Low without independent triangulation.
- **Contradicting evidence** lowers a rating; it is recorded alongside the finding rather than removed.

**9. Limitations**

**9.1 Scope boundaries**

- The research covers Layer 2 and interoperability demand only. Findings outside that scope are routed through the [Cross-RFP Handoff Memo](./CPC260015_M4_CrossRFPHandoffMemo_v2_11Oct2026.md) rather than analysed.
- It focuses on the African informal economy and the builders serving it. Findings are not extrapolated to other regions, and findings specific to one country are labelled as such.
- It does not design ecosystem funding mechanisms or prescribe technical roadmaps; forward-looking statements are sequencing recommendations.

**9.2 Access constraints**

- Category 3: one interview against five to eight. No wallet provider was interviewed.
- Category 2: five against six to ten.
- Category 4: ten against four to six, which strengthens the negative-case evidence but does not offset the Category 3 shortfall.
- No large African operator from the desk research list was interviewed.

**9.3 Respondent bias**

- **Familiarity bias.** Most Cardano builders interviewed chose Cardano through familiarity, a hackathon or community, not by comparing chains. Their views on alternatives are limited.
- **Recruitment through Cardano networks.** Category 1 was recruited largely through Cardano community and Catalyst networks, which favours active, engaged builders over those who left quietly. Category 4 partly offsets this.
- **Funding-seeking respondents.** Four respondents were flagged as seeking funding or constrained by it. Their claims are separated from independent demand evidence.
- **Provider self-interest.** Providers may describe barriers in ways that favour their own products. Provider claims are triangulated where possible and held at Low otherwise.
- **Our own interest.** The lead researcher's own validation work (the Zivana Protocol cases) is disclosed as conflicted evidence throughout, and independently reviewed.
- **Early-stage respondents.** Many respondents are early-stage, with few or no live users, so their choices show how chains are selected more than demand at scale.

**9.4 Other limitations**

- Many findings rest on one respondent and are labelled Low.
- Interviews were conducted by the research team; interviewer framing may have influenced some answers. Instruments were fixed in advance to limit this.
- AI tools were used in transcription, extraction, classification and drafting. Every cited extract is checked against its text, but with 500 extracts a manual review may not catch every issue.
