**Document Purpose**

This is the public summary of our final Blocked-Demand Case Register (D6). The full register, with every case's evidence, is published alongside it in redacted form.

**Main findings**

1. **Fifteen cases, eleven of which pass the Layer 2 or interoperability workflow test.** Four are real blockers caused by licensing, funding or liquidity, and are routed to the adjacent RFPs and workstreams that own them.
2. **The Layer 2 cases sit in infrastructure.** Oracle publication, order-book trading, micropayments, sensor data and fast transactions between parties not known in advance.
3. **The interoperability cases are about access.** Mobile wallet connection, phone-number payments and cross-chain bridging.
4. **An independent technical review confirmed four case claims and qualified five.** Three cases rose in confidence; two of our own validation cases changed because the capability they lacked now partly exists.

**Limitations**

- Provider-side cases are held at Low confidence: one bridge, interoperability or wallet provider was interviewed against a committed five to eight.
- Scale rests on what respondents reported.
- The remaining technical cases depend on roadmap items not yet on mainnet: Leios and CIP-0118 Nested Transactions (Dijkstra hard fork, tentatively December 2026 to March 2027) and Midgard (targeting the end of 2026).
