**Document Purpose**

This is the draft Evidence Threshold Framework (D9). It sets the minimum evidence a Layer 2 or interoperability proposal should show before it is funded, partnered or placed on a roadmap. It covers five dimensions: demand evidence, blocker severity, value-flow benefit, adoption pathway and measurable outcomes. It is built from what this research found credible and what it found insufficient. It answers Decision Gate 10.

It is scoped to Layer 2 and interoperability proposals only. It does not cover the design of ecosystem funding mechanisms more broadly.

**1. Summary**

1. **Demand must be independent and tied to a named workflow.** A proposal should show someone other than the proposer who wants to deploy and cannot, and name the workflow that is blocked.
2. **The blocker must survive the non-Layer 2 test.** Liquidity, wallet support, bridge or provider access, tooling, commercial uncertainty, compliance and partner access must each be ruled out before a blocker is treated as a Layer 2 blocker.
3. **Value-flow benefit must be shown, not assumed.** Technical possibility, roadmap existence and bridge volume without source and destination do not count.
4. **The adoption pathway must name who adopts, how they are reached, and what happens when any subsidy ends.** In this research, every observed adoption was subsidised, and both native oracle providers that lost their funding or paying clients stopped operating.
5. **Outcomes must be measured after delivery, not only at delivery.** Two funded items in this research were recorded as delivered and later shut down.

**2. How to Use This Framework**

- Check the proposal against the five minimum standards in Section 3. A proposal that misses any of them is not ready for "fund now".
- Use the table for the proposal's type in Section 4 for the specific evidence and measures expected.
- Use the signals in Section 5 to tell strong proposals from weak ones.
- Choose the decision using Section 6.

**4. Minimum Evidence by Proposal Type**

Follows RFP 07 Appendix G, with the adoption pathway added as a fifth dimension, as committed in our proposal.

| Proposal type | Minimum demand evidence | Minimum blocker evidence | Minimum value-flow evidence | Adoption pathway | Required measures |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Layer 2 | Named applications blocked at deployment, from builders other than the proposer | The seven non-Layer 2 alternatives ruled out; shown that base-layer upgrades due within twelve months would not resolve it | Settlement retained on Cardano; outflows stated | Named first users and the channel that reaches them | Applications deployed, transactions settled to Cardano, users retained, at set intervals after launch |
| Bridge | Named builders or operators who need the route, and the assets they would move | Shown that liquidity, wallet or provider access is not the real constraint | All six elements, with expected direction of flow; dependency risk stated | Who moves value, in which direction, and why it stays on Cardano | Volume with source, destination and retention; security incidents; uptime |
| Partner chain | Named applications or users on both sides | Shown why a partner chain rather than an application on Cardano | Retention mechanism for Cardano, such as stake pool security or settlement; lock-in or exit stated | How the partner chain's users reach Cardano | Two-way flows observed; stake pool participation; activity settling to Cardano |
| Wallet integration | Named applications and user groups needing the capability (for example mobile connection or phone-based access) | The user experience or access gap stated, and shown not to be a tooling or documentation gap only | Users and activity kept on Cardano rather than routed through another chain | The distribution channel that reaches the users, such as an existing app or network | Users onboarded and active over time; transactions from the new channel |
| Messaging | Named cross-chain workflows that need messages, not only asset transfer | eUTxO-specific integration cost stated | Direction of flow; dependency on the external provider | Applications that will use it first | Messages and applications using it after delivery |
| Other infrastructure (for example oracles) | Named applications that will pay, or that depend on the service | Commercial, coordination or capability gap identified | Benefit to Cardano applications; dependency stated | Revenue or funding after any subsidy ends; the effect on existing native providers | Operation and paying users after the funded period |

**5. Signals of Strong and Weak Proposals**

| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| Names the workflow, the applications and the users waiting | Describes a market or technology in general terms |
| Demand comes from others, preferably more than one evidence type | Demand comes only from the proposer, or from sentiment or events |
| Rules out liquidity, wallet, tooling, provider, commercial, compliance and partner access before claiming a Layer 2 need | Treats every blocker as technical |
| States outflows and dependency risk as well as inflows | Treats every connection as positive |
| Names the distribution channel to users | Assumes users will arrive once capability exists |
| Explains how operation continues after any subsidy | Depends on continued free access or grants without a plan |
| States the provider's continuity risk and current status with a date | Relies on announcements or stale status |
| Commits to measures after delivery | Measures only milestone completion |
| Has independent technical review where claims are complex | Relies on self-attestation |

**6. Choosing the Decision**

Decisions use our action set: fund now, coordinate, partner, monitor, defer or reject (RFP 07 Appendix G lists "no action" in place of reject). Value-flow consequences follow RFP 07.

| Decision | When |
|---|---|
| Fund now | All five minimum standards are met, and the value-flow evidence shows benefit coming into Cardano. |
| Partner | Two-way benefit is shown for Cardano and another ecosystem. |
| Coordinate | The blocker is coordination, documentation or access rather than missing capability, and can be resolved without new engineering funding. |
| Monitor | Connectivity improves but adoption impact is unclear or limited, or delivery is pending. |
| Defer | Dependency risk is high without safeguards, or a dependency is not operating. |
| Reject | Value would mainly flow out of Cardano without compensating benefit, unless mitigations or the strategic rationale are strong. |

**8. How the Desk Research Proposals Were Tested**

Desk research recorded four design directions to test in primary research ([Desk Research Summary](https://github.com/Nextrium/cpc-product-website/blob/milestone-1-deliverables/docs/product-research/nextrium/rfp-07-deliverables/CPC260015_M1_DeskResearchSummary_v1_01Aug2026.md) Section 7.5). The interviews tested them as follows.

| Direction | What the interviews showed | Included in this framework as |
|---|---|---|
| Track record extended to operational continuity | Two native oracle providers stopped operating after years of service and funding (E-075, E-096) | Adoption pathway: provider continuity risk |
| Outcome measurement after the final milestone | Seven builders and providers said funded integrations had not changed what they build ([Negative-Case and Non-Cardano Evidence Summary](./CPC260015_M3_NegativeCaseNonCardanoEvidenceSummary_v1_10Oct2026.md), Section 4) | Measurable outcomes after delivery |
| Sustainability after any subsidy ends | One builder declined a free oracle because he could not pay afterwards (E-335); another would pay if the service is worth it (E-494); a provider expects small clients to drop out when subsidies end (E-119) | Adoption pathway: operation after subsidy |
| Independent technical verification | Technical claims in this research, on throughput, integration effort and wallet connection, were sent for independent technical review by Samir Idris, which is pending | Strong-proposal signal: independent technical review |

**10. Limitations**

- The framework is drawn from one research study focused on African emerging markets, and should be tested against other markets.
- The proposal-type tables state the kind of evidence required, not numeric targets. This research did not find a basis for setting numeric thresholds.
- Provider-side findings that inform the bridge, messaging and partner-chain rows are Low confidence, because one provider was interviewed against a committed five to eight.
