**Purpose and Use**

Five interview instruments and one respondent screener covering all four respondent categories. Category 3 is split into two variants because bridge, messaging and stablecoin providers and wallet providers require materially different conversations.

Every question maps to at least one of the seven research hypotheses or eleven decision gates. Section purposes are stated in italics for the interviewer and are not read to the respondent.

A single interview protocol applies to all categories and is stated once in the following section rather than repeated in each instrument. Category-specific recording requirements are stated at the end of each instrument.

| **Instrument**       | **Applies to**                                                   | **Duration**     |
|----------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------|
| Respondent Screener  | All categories, before scheduling                                | 5 to 10 minutes  |
| Category 1           | African informal economy builders and operators on Cardano       | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Category 2           | Infrastructure, tooling, identity and application protocol teams | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Category 3 Variant A | Bridges, messaging protocols and stablecoin issuers              | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Category 3 Variant B | Wallet providers                                                 | 45 to 60 minutes |
| Category 4           | Non-Cardano and negative-case respondents                        | 45 to 60 minutes |

**Interview Protocol**

This protocol applies to every interview in every category. It is stated once here and is not repeated within the instruments.

**Before the Interview**

- Read the consent disclosure statement in full. Do not paraphrase or abbreviate it.

- Confirm and log attribution status: named, anonymised, or confidential. Do not proceed to any research question until this is recorded.

- Request recording consent separately and explicitly. If declined, do not record.

- Record whether the respondent is seeking Cardano funding or integration support, for evidence separation during analysis.

**During the Interview**

- Where a respondent claims an application is blocked by missing Layer 2 capability, test and eliminate the seven non-Layer 2 alternatives before recording it as such: liquidity, wallet support, bridge or provider access, tooling, commercial uncertainty, compliance, and partner access.

- Do not characterise any provider status change, exploit, milestone position, funding position or public announcement to a respondent.

- Do not imply that a respondent who selected infrastructure other than Cardano made an error.

- Section purposes shown in italics are interviewer guidance and are not read aloud.

**After the Interview**

- Confirm attribution status before ending the call.

- File structured notes in the correct category folder within 24 hours.

- Classify every barrier raised against the nine-type taxonomy before filing.

- Assign a confidence level to each finding proportionate to source count and corroboration.

- Record findings that Cardano infrastructure does not meet a respondent need accurately and completely. Do not soften, minimise or omit them.

- Update the Consent Tracking Register with interview date and confirmed attribution status.

**Binding Sequencing Constraints**

Two constraints are binding and override interviewer discretion.

- The consent disclosure statement precedes every research question without exception.

- The open capability question precedes any mention of the Layer 2 inventory. If a respondent has already named initiatives from the inventory earlier in the conversation, the question is skipped or marked as prompted, and confidence is reduced accordingly.

**1. Respondent Screener**

Administered before scheduling, by message or short call. Confirms category, relevance and eligibility without biasing later responses. Does not mention Layer 2, specific initiatives, or any finding from desk research.

**Purpose**

Three outcomes: confirm the respondent belongs in a category, identify which category and which instrument applies, and establish whether they meet the relevance threshold for an interview slot.

**Screening Questions**

S1. What does your organisation build or operate?

S2. Which blockchain or blockchains does it run on today?

S3. Do you serve users or builders in African markets? Which markets?

S4. Is your product live, in testing, or in development?

S5. Roughly how many users, builders or transactions does it handle?

S6. Are you currently seeking funding or integration support from the Cardano ecosystem?

S7. Who would be the right person to speak with, and are they available in the next two weeks?

**Category Assignment**

| **Answer pattern**                                                                 | **Category** | **Instrument** |
|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Builds on Cardano, serves African end users or informal economy participants       | 1            | Category 1     |
| Builds infrastructure, tooling, identity or protocol on Cardano for other builders | 2            | Category 2     |
| Provides bridge, messaging or stablecoin issuance touching Cardano                 | 3            | Variant A      |
| Provides a Cardano wallet                                                          | 3            | Variant B      |
| Builds on a chain other than Cardano, serves African markets                       | 4            | Category 4     |
| No African market relevance and no Cardano relationship                            | Excluded     | None           |

**Eligibility and Recording**

A respondent is eligible where S1 to S3 establish both a chain relationship and African market relevance. Where S3 returns no African relevance, the respondent is excluded unless they are a Category 2 or Category 3 infrastructure provider, for whom African relevance is not required.

A yes to S6 is recorded as the funding-seeking flag in the Consent Tracking Register. It does not exclude a respondent. It determines that their claims are separated from independent demand evidence during analysis.

**2. Category 1: African Builders and Operators on Cardano**

**Section A: Opening and Context**

A1. Tell me about what you are building. Who is it for and what problem does it solve?

A2. Why did you choose Cardano as your infrastructure? What were the alternatives you considered?

A3. How far along is the deployment? Are you on testnet, mainnet, or still in development?

**Section B: What Is Working**

*Establishes a positive baseline before blockers, and surfaces genuine adoption signals.*

B1. What has worked well on Cardano for what you are building? Anything that was easier than you expected?

**Section C: Blockers and Workarounds**

*Identifies blocked demand cases. The seven non-Layer 2 alternatives must be tested before any blocker is classified.*

C1. What is the hardest thing you have tried to do on Cardano that you could not do, or could only do with significant difficulty?

C2. Have you had to build a workaround for something? What was it, and what would you have preferred to use?

C3. Is there anything you planned to include in your application that you had to drop because the infrastructure was not available?

C4. For anything blocking you: how long has it been a blocker, and what would it take for you to move forward?

**Section D: Capability and Infrastructure**

*D1 must precede D2. D1 surfaces undocumented capability absences and prompting defeats it.*

D1. Is there a capability you needed and could not find anywhere on Cardano? Take your time, I am not looking for a specific answer.

D2. Are you aware of any of the following developments on Cardano in the past six months: the Midgard optimistic rollup targeting mainnet at end of 2026, the LayerZero cross-chain messaging integration, and the USDCx stablecoin launch in February?

*Record yes or no for each. This tests whether delivered infrastructure reached this builder.*

D3. If Cardano base-layer throughput increased by thirty to sixty-five times its current level within the next twelve months, would that change anything for you? Would it resolve any of the blockers you mentioned?

D4. What is your current per-transaction cost for the workflow you described? How does that compare to alternatives you know of?

D5. Have you paid for any infrastructure services on Cardano, such as API access, oracle feeds, or hosted node services? Under what conditions would you pay for infrastructure?

**Section E: Distribution and Reach**

*Tests whether the constraint is reach and cost rather than capability.*

E1. Who are your end users and how do they access your application? What device, what connectivity, what level of prior blockchain experience?

E2. Have you encountered wallet or access constraints? Anything your users could not do because of how the wallet works or what it requires?

E3. If you needed to reach users on a basic phone without internet access, what would you need Cardano to provide that it does not today?

**Section F: Identity and Data**

F1. Does your application need to verify who someone is? How are you handling that today?

F2. Many African countries are currently updating or consolidating their national identity systems. Does your application interact with any government-issued identity, and if so, what does that create in terms of demand or constraint for what you are building?

F3. If your users need to prove something about themselves to access your application or a service, what do they currently use and what would you rather they had?

**Section G: Ecosystem and Governance**

G1. Are you aware that the Cardano ecosystem funded a set of critical integrations last year covering stablecoins, oracles, bridges and analytics? Has any of that changed what you are building or planning?

G2. Some of those integrations were delivered with free access periods. The oracle integration launched with one year of free access. When that free period ends, would you continue using it at a commercial rate? What would make you say yes or no?

**Section H: Closing**

H1. If you could change one thing about Cardano infrastructure to make your application viable or faster to build, what would it be?

H2. Is there anyone else working in this space that you think we should speak with?

H3. Is there anything you wanted to say that I have not asked about?

**Category-specific recording**

Blocked use cases, workarounds, capability gaps named, and referrals.

**3. Category 2: Infrastructure, Tooling, Identity and Protocol Teams**

*Category note: identify which layer this respondent covers before starting, being oracle, identity, API, managed infrastructure, Layer 2, agent infrastructure, or core protocol. D5 is the sequencing-constrained question in this instrument.*

**Section A: Opening and Context**

A1. Tell me about what your team builds and who uses it on Cardano.

A2. How long have you been building on or for Cardano, and what drew you to it originally?

A3. What is the current status of your product or service? Live on mainnet, in testnet, or in active development?

**Section B: Builder Demand You Are Seeing**

*Category 2 respondents observe builder demand at scale, which is more representative than any individual builder interview.*

B1. Who are the builders using your product, and what are they building? Are there patterns in the use cases you see?

B2. What do builders most commonly ask you for that you cannot currently provide?

B3. Have you seen builders leave your product or move to different infrastructure because of something you do not support? What was it?

B4. Are there workflows or use cases you know builders want to support on Cardano that your infrastructure cannot currently serve?

**Section C: Integration and Technical Constraints**

C1. What is the hardest part of building on or integrating with Cardano from your position? What takes longer or costs more than it should?

C2. Is there anything in Cardano core infrastructure that creates a constraint on what you can offer? Something you have to work around rather than use directly?

C3. We found that documentation for deploying some Cardano stacks beyond a basic quick-start configuration was effectively absent. Does that pattern exist in the layer you work in, and what has the effect been?

C4. Are there other components in the Cardano stack you depend on and cannot influence? Has that dependency caused problems?

**Section D: Layer 2 and Interoperability**

D1. Do builders using your product face blockers related to throughput, latency or transaction cost? What do they tell you?

D2. If Cardano base-layer throughput increased by thirty to sixty-five times its current level within the next twelve months, what would change for builders using your product?

D3. Are there cross-chain or interoperability requirements you hear from builders that your product cannot address? What are they?

D4. Is there a Layer 2 or scaling capability that does not exist on Cardano today that would materially change what you can offer?

D5. Are there Cardano Layer 2 or scaling initiatives relevant to your work that you think are missing from the ecosystem? Anything that should exist but does not?

**Section E: Commercial and Sustainability**

E1. How do builders pay for your product or service today? Is it free, subsidised, or commercially priced?

E2. Have you found that builders on Cardano are willing to pay for infrastructure? What has your experience been?

E3. If a subsidy or grant ended, what happens to the builders who depend on your product? Do they pay, find an alternative, or stop?

E4. What would make your product commercially sustainable on Cardano at current ecosystem scale?

**Section F: Critical Integrations Awareness**

F1. Are you aware that the Cardano ecosystem funded a set of critical integrations covering stablecoins, oracles, bridges and analytics? Has any of that changed the context for what you are building?

F2. One of those integrations arrived with a funded free-access period for builders. When that period ends and commercial pricing applies, what do you expect to happen among the builders you serve?

**Section G: Identity and Access**

*Ask only where relevant to the respondent layer. Skip otherwise.*

G1. Do any of the builders using your product need to verify user identity or issue verifiable credentials? How are they handling that today?

G2. Many African countries are currently updating or consolidating their national identity systems. Is there any downstream demand that creates for builders using your infrastructure?

G3. Identity infrastructure on Cardano currently requires a full node stack for credential issuance, while resolution can be done through a public lightweight resolver. Does that asymmetry affect any builders you work with?

**Section H: Ecosystem and Coordination**

H1. Is there something that would make Cardano a more viable platform for the builders you serve, that is not in your own power to provide?

H2. Are there coordination problems in the Cardano ecosystem that affect your work? Things requiring multiple parties to act together that are not happening?

H3. Is there anything happening in the Cardano ecosystem right now that concerns you about its direction or sustainability?

H4. Is there anyone else you think we should speak with, on the builder side or among infrastructure providers?

H5. Is there anything you wanted to say that I have not asked about?

**Category-specific recording**

Builder demand patterns, capability gaps named from the provider position, commercial viability signals, and coordination blockers.

**4. Category 3 Variant A: Bridges, Messaging and Stablecoin Issuers**

*Category note: record whether this respondent operates a bridge, a messaging protocol, or stablecoin issuance.*

**Section A: Opening and Context**

A1. Tell me about your product and how it relates to Cardano.

A2. How did Cardano come onto your roadmap? Was it driven by demand, a strategic decision, or something else?

A3. What is the current status of your Cardano integration or deployment?

**Section B: The Cardano Integration Experience**

B1. What did integrating with Cardano require from your team technically? How did it compare to integrating with other chains you support?

B2. Were there things in Cardano architecture that required you to rework approaches you use elsewhere, rather than port them directly?

B3. What documentation, tooling or support did you find, and what was missing?

B4. Were there Cardano-specific primitives or components that were available but that your team was not immediately familiar with?

B5. How long did the Cardano integration take relative to comparable chains? What drove that timeline?

**Section C: Demand and Commercial Factors**

C1. What was the demand signal that led you to prioritise Cardano? Where did it come from?

C2. Who uses your Cardano integration today, and for what?

C3. What does commercial viability look like for your Cardano integration? Is the demand there to sustain it without subsidy?

C4. If you were evaluating Cardano as a new integration today, what would you need to see to proceed?

**Section D: What Cardano Needs**

D1. What capability on Cardano would most improve your ability to serve users through your product?

D2. Are there features you have on other chains that you cannot currently offer on Cardano? What are they and what causes the gap?

D3. Is there an interoperability feature type you hear demand for that Cardano cannot support today: asset bridging, message passing, liquidity routing, settlement interoperability, or something else?

D4. What would make Cardano a higher priority for your roadmap going forward?

**Section E: African and Emerging Market Context**

E1. Do you have users in African markets? What are they using your product for?

E2. Have you seen demand patterns in African markets that differ from other regions?

E3. Are there access or cost constraints in African markets that your product encounters, either for you or for the builders who integrate with you?

**Section F: Value Flow and Dependency**

F1. When a user moves value through your product involving Cardano, where does that value go? Does it return to Cardano or move elsewhere?

F2. Does your product create a dependency on Cardano for its users, or does it make it easier to move away?

F3. If your Cardano integration were discontinued, what would your users do? Would they stay on Cardano through another route or leave?

**Section G: Closing**

G1. Is there a coordination or ecosystem problem affecting your Cardano work that is not in your power to solve alone?

G2. Is there anyone else we should speak with on bridges, interoperability or stablecoin infrastructure?

G3. Is there anything you wanted to say that I have not asked about?

**Category-specific recording**

Integration cost evidence, demand signals, commercial viability indicators, capability gaps and value-flow direction observed. Apply value-flow classification using all six required elements: expected inflows, potential outflows, retention mechanism, dependency risk, evidence and confidence.

**5. Category 3 Variant B: Wallet Providers**

*Category note: record whether the wallet is a browser extension, a mobile application, or both.*

**Section A: Opening and Context**

A1. Tell me about your wallet and who uses it.

A2. What markets are your users concentrated in, and where is adoption growing?

**Section B: Access Channel and Onboarding**

*Confirms and quantifies the access-channel gap, and identifies whether any wallet has moved toward phone-number addressing or USSD.*

B1. What is the minimum viable device and connectivity level for your wallet today?

B2. A user sending a stablecoin on Cardano currently needs to know a bech32 address. What alternatives to address-based sending have you considered or implemented?

B3. Have you explored USSD or any channel that works without internet access? What was the outcome?

B4. What specifically stops users from completing a transaction, and how often does that happen?

**Section C: Fee and Collateral Experience**

C1. A user holding only stablecoins cannot post the ADA collateral required for smart contract interaction. How does your wallet handle that?

C2. Users who receive many small payments accumulate fragmented UTxOs and face rising transaction costs over time. Do you manage UTxO consolidation for users, and what does that cost them?

C3. What do your users tell you about Cardano transaction costs compared to what they use elsewhere?

**Section D: Cross-Chain and Infrastructure**

D1. What cross-chain functionality do your users ask for that you cannot currently offer?

D2. What Cardano API or infrastructure services does your wallet depend on, and have outages or changes to those services affected your users?

**Section E: African Market Specifics**

E1. Celo MiniPay reached 16 million wallets primarily through embedding in Opera Mini. What is your distribution strategy for African markets, and what has worked?

E2. What do African users need from a wallet that differs materially from users in other markets?

E3. Many African countries are consolidating national identity systems. Are your users asking to verify identity or credentials through the wallet, and what are you doing about that?

**Section F: Commercial and Sustainability**

F1. How does your wallet sustain itself at current Cardano ecosystem scale?

F2. Since USDCx launched in February, has stablecoin usage in your wallet changed? What are you seeing?

F3. What distribution partner or integration would most change your growth trajectory in African markets?

**Section G: Closing**

G1. What one infrastructure or ecosystem change would most help your users?

G2. Is there anyone else we should speak with on wallet access or user experience?

G3. Anything you wanted to say that I have not asked?

**Category-specific recording**

Access channel constraints confirmed or refuted, fee and collateral barrier evidence, cross-chain requirements, infrastructure dependencies and African market signals. Note whether constraints are Cardano-specific or industry-wide.

**6. Category 4: Non-Cardano and Negative-Case Respondents**

*Category note: record which chain or infrastructure this respondent builds on.*

**Section A: Opening and Context**

A1. Tell me about what you are building and who it is for.

A2. What infrastructure are you building on, and what drove that choice?

**Section B: The Chain Selection Decision**

*The core question for this category and the primary reason for the interview.*

B1. Was Cardano evaluated before you settled on your current infrastructure? At what point and by whom?

B2. What would Cardano have needed to offer for it to be in serious contention?

B3. Were there specific technical, commercial or ecosystem factors that ruled it out? What were they?

B4. Has anything changed in the Cardano ecosystem recently that has caused you to reconsider, or that you are watching?

**Section C: What Your Infrastructure Gives You**

C1. What does your current infrastructure give you that you could not get elsewhere, including on Cardano?

C2. What has been harder than expected on your current chain?

C3. Are there things your users or builders ask for that your current infrastructure cannot support?

**Section D: African Market Requirements**

D1. Who are your users in African markets and what are they doing with your product?

D2. What infrastructure requirements did serving African markets impose that you had not anticipated?

D3. Transaction cost is a live competitive factor in African payments. What does your cost structure look like for a small-value stablecoin transfer, and how does that compare to what your users tell you they pay elsewhere?

D4. How do your users access your product? What device, what connectivity, what level of prior experience?

**Section E: Interoperability and Integration**

E1. What cross-chain or interoperability features do your users need? How are you providing them?

E2. Are there integration patterns you use across chains that Cardano architecture makes difficult or impossible to port directly?

**Section F: What Would Change Your View**

F1. Under what conditions, if any, would you consider adding Cardano as a supported chain?

F2. Is there something Cardano could do in the next twelve months that would change your assessment?

F3. Are there builders or users pushing you toward Cardano that you are not currently able to serve there?

**Section G: Closing**

G1. Is there anyone else we should speak with who evaluated Cardano and chose differently?

G2. Anything you wanted to say that I have not asked?

**Category-specific recording**

Stated reasons for not selecting Cardano, specific capability or commercial gaps named, African market requirements observed, and any conditions stated for future Cardano consideration. Do not adjust confidence level on negative findings.

**7. Hypothesis and Decision Gate Coverage**

Every instrument maps to hypotheses and decision gates. Coverage is uneven by design: each category is the primary evidence source for different gates.

| **Instrument**       | **Primary hypotheses** | **Primary decision gates** |
|----------------------|------------------------|----------------------------|
| Category 1           | H1, H2, H3             | 1, 2, 3, 4                 |
| Category 2           | H2, H3, H4, H6         | 2, 3, 5, 9                 |
| Category 3 Variant A | H4, H5, H6             | 5, 6, 7, 8, 9              |
| Category 3 Variant B | H1, H3, H4             | 1, 3, 5                    |
| Category 4           | H3, H5, H6             | 6, 7, 9                    |

Decision Gate 10 on evidence thresholds is informed across all categories through the questions on willingness to pay and post-subsidy behaviour. Decision Gate 11 on cross-RFP handoff is informed by any finding routed outside this scope during analysis.
