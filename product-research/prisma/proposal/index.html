2. Executive Summary​

The Cardano Product Committee has identified government and emerging-market entry as one of the most strategically significant — and most under-evidenced — areas in the current portfolio. This proposal addresses that gap.

Cardano's challenge is not simply to identify promising emerging markets. The deeper problem is to establish a repeatable system for discovering, validating, prioritising, and operationalising high-probability adoption opportunities across diverse regional ecosystems. Broad geographic labels such as Africa, Latin America, or East Asia offer limited decision guidance. Adoption opportunities emerge not from geography alone, but from the intersection of stakeholder demand, institutional readiness, regulatory conditions, delivery capacity, infrastructure availability, and local ecosystem dynamics.

This research will produce evidence-based regional entry playbooks for Cardano — decision tools designed to enable the Product Committee and ecosystem stakeholders to distinguish actionable opportunities from cosmetic engagement, prioritise regions based on transparent criteria, and identify the conditions that must be satisfied before ecosystem resources are deployed.

Research Framing​

The research is framed around a single master question:

***Which regional ecosystem configurations create the highest-probability pathway for ***

***sustained Cardano adoption, and what evidence thresholds must be satisfied before ***

ecosystem resources are deployed?

This framing deliberately shifts from "which countries should Cardano enter" — a question that typically generates country lists rather than decisions — to "how does Cardano build a structured, evidence-based capability for regional ecosystem development." The distinction is material: the former produces descriptive reports; the latter produces a repeatable intelligence system.

Scope Architecture​

The proposal addresses three RFPs with a clear hierarchy of scope:

•​ Primary — RFP 05 (Government and Emerging Markets Entry Strategy): Regional opportunity screening, entry playbooks, regulatory mapping, counterparty intelligence, infrastructure prerequisite assessment, and adoption signal framework. Approximately 20% of research effort. •​ Supporting — RFP 01 (Customer Segmentation): Stakeholder segmentation and partner/user archetype analysis where necessary to define counterparty pathways. Approximately 10% of research effort.

3. Understanding of the Brief​

3.1 The Decision Problem​

The RFP does not ask for a market survey. It asks for a decision architecture. The evidence gap the CPC identifies is not simply that Cardano lacks information about emerging markets — it is that Cardano stakeholders cannot currently distinguish between regional opportunities that warrant action and those that merely appear promising. Without that distinction, ecosystem resources risk flowing toward geographic narratives rather than viable entry plays.

The RFP is explicit about this concern. It warns against treating "LATAM," "Africa," or "East Asia" as coherent markets, against confusing event attendance or letters of intent with adoption signal, and against producing regional recommendations that cannot be connected to reachable counterparties, realistic regulatory pathways, and available delivery capacity. Every one of these failure modes reflects the same underlying problem: evidence that was descriptive rather than decision-grade.

This proposal is designed to produce decision-grade evidence.

3.2 The Eight Decision Gates​

The RFP defines eight explicit decision gates that the research must answer. Each gate corresponds to a specific question that Cardano stakeholders need to resolve before committing resources to a regional opportunity. The research design in this proposal is built around those gates, not around a pre-selected country list or a predetermined conclusion.

The eight gates, in summary, are:

•​ Which regions should be prioritised for near-term government and emerging-market entry, based on comparative evidence rather than strategic narrative? •​ Which use cases are credible entry plays in each priority region, and which are aspirational without current evidence of viability? •​ Which counterparties are reachable within a 12–24 month horizon, and what route exists to decision-makers or implementation partners? •​ What does the regulatory landscape look like per priority region, and what dependencies or blockers affect the entry play? •​ What infrastructure prerequisites must be in place before action is credible, including stablecoin liquidity, off-ramp access, identity, privacy, mobile-first design, and delivery capacity? •​ What partner and delivery model is required in each region, and who can build, operate, support, and scale the solution? •​ What constitutes meaningful adoption in each play, and what should not be counted as evidence of progress? •​ Which findings belong in adjacent RFPs or workstreams, and what cross-portfolio handoffs are required to keep scope clean?

The methodology described in Section 6, the workplan in Section 14, and the deliverables in Section 15 are each mapped directly to these eight gates. No section of the research exists except to answer one or more of them.

4. Strategic Context and Problem Definition​

4.1 Cardano's Current Moment​

Cardano has entered the Voltaire era of its development with on-chain governance now fully operational. The Cardano 2030 vision and the 2026 strategic roadmap reflect an ecosystem that is shifting from protocol construction to adoption and ecosystem development. The CPC's research portfolio is a direct expression of that shift: the questions being asked are no longer primarily technical, they are strategic and commercial.

In that context, government and emerging-market entry is not a peripheral concern. Several of the strongest early signals of Cardano adoption have come from developing-market contexts — agricultural supply chain, humanitarian payment infrastructure, identity, land registry, and mobile-first financial inclusion use cases. These signals have not yet been systematically evaluated, validated, or translated into a sequenced entry strategy.

The risk, as the RFP correctly identifies, is that Cardano continues to engage with "emerging markets" as a strategic aspiration rather than a structured programme. Aspirational language about Africa, Latin America, or Southeast Asia is not the same as a decision about which specific ecosystem cluster should receive attention next, which use case should lead, which partners must be engaged, and what prerequisites must be satisfied before a pilot is credible.

4.2 Why Market-Entry Research Often Fails​

Most market-entry research for blockchain networks produces one of two outputs: a country report or a technology narrative. Country reports catalogue national economic indicators, regulatory environments, and sector statistics at a level of generality that is rarely actionable. Technology narratives describe how blockchain could solve land registry, remittance, or identity problems without specifying who must implement the solution, what institutional pathway leads to a funded pilot, or what evidence would confirm that the opportunity is real rather than plausible.

Neither approach answers the questions Cardano actually needs answered. The country-report approach fails because most decision constraints do not operate at the national level — they operate at the level of specific use cases, counterparty relationships, and institutional configurations. The technology-narrative approach fails because it begins from the blockchain and works backwards to a problem, rather than beginning from a problem and testing whether blockchain is the right instrument.

This proposal adopts a different approach. It begins from the question of which regional ecosystem configurations — defined by the intersection of problem severity, stakeholder demand, institutional readiness, regulatory conditions, and delivery capacity — create credible adoption pathways. The research does not begin with a predetermined geography or a predetermined conclusion. It begins with a framework for comparing candidate opportunities and a commitment to testing each hypothesis against primary evidence.

The RFP is unambiguous on a concern that has appeared repeatedly in Cardano's emerging-market history: the risk of confusing visibility with adoption. Event attendance, conference panels, letters of intent, informal government interest, and social media engagement are not adoption signals. They are

5. Proposal Thesis and Institutional Positioning​

5.1 Proposal Thesis​

The central thesis of this proposal is:

Cardano's government and emerging-market entry challenge is not primarily a question of geography, but of evidence architecture. The research will generate decision-ready regional entry playbooks grounded in primary evidence, a structured evidence-generation framework reusable beyond this research cycle, and a set of decision criteria that allow Cardano stakeholders to distinguish viable entry plays from aspirational narratives — before resources are deployed.

This thesis directly responds to the CPC's evidence-gap statement. It does not assume that the answer is already known. It treats the research as a genuine investigation whose conclusions will be determined by evidence, not confirmed by assumption.

5.2 What PRISMA Is​

PRISMA is a distributed action-learning and field-evidence organisation. It is not a consulting firm, market research agency, blockchain advocacy group, or grant distribution mechanism. PRISMA's value lies in a specific and unusual combination: the ability to generate primary evidence from real-world practitioners, grassroots ecosystem actors, hub networks, and institutional intermediaries — and to structure that evidence into decision-useful outputs for larger organisations that cannot easily access those actors directly.

PRISMA's work sits at the intersection of ecosystem development, venture development, evaluation, systems mapping, action learning, and implementation research. 5.3 Action Learning Journeys​

PRISMA's core methodology is the Action Learning Journey (ALJ). ALJs are structured evidence-generation environments — not workshops, not conferences — in which practitioners, ecosystem actors, venture teams, institutions, and community participants collectively investigate real-world problems through coordinated learning and action.

ALJs produce structured access to implementation realities, stakeholder perspectives, capability gaps, partnership opportunities, adoption barriers, and ecosystem dynamics. They are documented through multi-perspective case studies that convert distributed field knowledge into public evidence. The methodology has been applied across multiple regions and contexts, with case-study outputs published at case.prisma.events.

For this research, ALJs serve a specific function: they provide a structured method for generating primary evidence from grassroots and institutional actors in target regions — access that cannot be replicated through remote interviews or desk research alone.

6. Research Design and Methodology​

6.1 Research Design Philosophy​

The research follows a structured mixed-method design. It begins with a desk-research phase to establish hypotheses and a screening baseline, proceeds through primary evidence collection to test and revise those hypotheses, and concludes with a synthesis phase that converts tested evidence into decision-ready frameworks and playbooks.

The design is explicitly hypothesis-testing rather than hypothesis-confirming. The research will investigate candidate regional opportunities without assuming in advance that any particular region is the correct answer. Regions, sub-regions, or country clusters that fail to demonstrate evidence-based viability will be recommended for deferral, monitoring, or no-go, not stretched to meet a predetermined conclusion.

The methodology is structured across seven workstreams. These workstreams operate in parallel during the primary research phase rather than sequentially, to ensure that regulatory findings, infrastructure assessments, and counterparty mapping can inform each other in real time.

6.2 Phase Architecture​

Phase 1 — Inception and Research Design (Weeks 1–3): Kickoff, workplan alignment, decision gate review, regional candidate list construction, screening criteria finalisation, ALJ design for primary research phase, stakeholder recruitment plan, interview guide development, bias control protocols.

Phase 2 — Regional Screening and Desk Research (Weeks 4–6): Application of the Regional Adoption Readiness Framework to all candidate regional clusters. Initial scores based on publicly available evidence. Production of the preliminary market prioritisation matrix. Identification of priority clusters for deep-dive primary research.

Phase 3 — Primary Research and Field Access (Weeks 7–12): Stakeholder interviews, expert calls, ALJ facilitation in selected hub locations, local partner engagement, regulatory mapping, infrastructure assessment, and counterparty mapping. Minimum of 30–45 primary research interactions across respondent types.

Phase 4 — Analysis and Framework Development (Weeks 13–15): Cross-evidence synthesis, RARF score refinement, CASS classification of identified signals, playbook drafting, regulatory summary drafting, counterparty access register compilation.

Phase 5 — Drafting and Review (Weeks 15–17): Full draft of all core deliverables, CPC review, validation against decision gates, revision of findings where access or evidence constraints require recalibration.

Phase 6 — Final Delivery (Week 18): Final report, executive decision memo, public summary, cross-RFP handoff memo, presentation to CPC.

The framework prevents both under-analysis (treating a region as viable without testing the components that determine viability) and over-analysis (spending primary research time on opportunities that fail basic screening criteria).

6.6 Use-Case Assessment Framework​

Use-case assessment for each priority region will evaluate:

•​ Problem Severity: Is the problem the use case addresses genuinely acute in this region? Is there evidence of unmet demand from local actors? •​ Cardano Fit: Does the specific use case require properties that Cardano provides — deterministic settlement, transparent public ledger, identity primitives, privacy features — or would a centralised or conventional solution serve equally well? •​ Competitive Alternatives: What alternatives currently exist — both blockchain and non-blockchain — and why or why not would Cardano be preferred? •​ Stakeholder Demand: Have local actors — not Cardano advocates — expressed specific demand for the kind of solution this use case represents? •​ Dependencies: Does the use case require stablecoin liquidity, off-ramp access, privacy, identity infrastructure, delivery partners, or regulatory approval before it is actionable? •​ Adoption Pathway: Is there a concrete pathway from initial pilot to recurring usage, and who owns each step of that pathway?

6.7 Regulatory Mapping Methodology​

Regulatory mapping will be conducted at a decision-oriented, landscape level appropriate for informing go/no-go, further diligence, and local counsel recommendations. It is not intended to replace formal legal advice, and this distinction will be clearly stated in all deliverables.

For each priority region, the regulatory mapping will identify:

•​ Relevant regulatory bodies and their posture toward blockchain, digital assets, payments, and data •​ Applicable licensing, permission, or registration requirements for candidate use cases •​ Stablecoin, cryptocurrency, and payment regulatory status •​ Data protection, privacy, and public-chain transparency considerations •​ Public procurement rules and pathways •​ Foreign exchange and capital control implications •​ Uncertainty levels and recommended further diligence actions

Where local-language primary research is required, this will be documented and the limitation noted where full coverage is not possible. •​ Elite and Capital-City Bias: The ALJ methodology deliberately surfaces grassroots and implementation-level actors who are typically absent from capital-city-focused research. This is one of the distinctive advantages of PRISMA's approach. •​ Access Overclaiming: All counterparty access will be classified as Interviewed, Warm Access, Referred, Desk-Identified, or Hypothetical. Findings based on Hypothetical or Desk-Identified sources will be clearly marked with lower confidence levels. •​ Treating Intent as Adoption: The CASS framework is the operational control for this risk. No signal will be classified above its evidence-supported tier.

11. Regulatory Mapping Plan​

11.1 Approach​

Regulatory mapping will be decision-oriented and proportionate. The objective is to identify the regulatory dependencies and blockers that affect whether a specific use case can proceed in a specific region — not to produce a comprehensive legal review.

The regulatory mapping will not constitute legal advice. Where findings indicate that a legal or regulatory pathway requires specialist review before action, this will be stated explicitly. PRISMA is not a legal advisory organisation, and will not represent regulatory findings with a level of certainty beyond what publicly available evidence and expert interview evidence can support.

13. Adoption Signal Plan​

13.1 CASS Application Protocol​

The Cardano Adoption Signal Standard (CASS) introduced in Section 6.4 will be applied consistently across all evidence collected during the research. The following protocol governs how CASS classifications are assigned:

•​ Every adoption signal identified during primary or desk research will be assigned a CASS tier at the time it is recorded •​ Signals that are currently cited in Cardano community discussion will be reviewed and reclassified where the evidence basis does not support the tier implied by prior community reporting •​ Tier 2 and above classifications require at least one primary evidence source — they cannot be assigned based on desk research alone •​ Tier 1 classification requires multiple corroborating primary sources and documentary evidence of recurring usage •​ All CASS classifications will be included in the evidence register with their source, date, and confidence level

13.2 Cosmetic Signal Identification​

The research will explicitly identify signals that have been cited as adoption evidence but do not meet CASS Tier 2 or above standards. Where the research finds that a prior community claim about regional progress represents Tier 4 (Weak Signal) evidence, this will be reported clearly, with the basis for the reclassification explained.

This is not a negative assessment of Cardano's regional activities — it is a necessary function of responsible research. Resources should not be deployed based on inflated signal assessments.

13.3 Signal-to-Action Translation​

The Research-to-Action Roadmap will translate CASS classifications into action recommendations:

•​ Tier 1 (Verified Adoption): Document and publish as evidence; identify what would scale the opportunity further •​ Tier 2 (Strong Signal): Recommend go or conditional-go; specify conditions and owner •​ Tier 3 (Moderate Signal): Recommend conditional-go with specific dependency resolution required; specify next evidence milestone •​ Tier 4 (Weak Signal): Recommend monitoring; specify what evidence upgrade would justify further investment

2. Executive Summary​

The Cardano Product Committee has identified government and emerging-market entry as one of the most strategically significant — and most under-evidenced — areas in the current portfolio. This proposal addresses that gap.

Cardano's challenge is not simply to identify promising emerging markets. The deeper problem is to establish a repeatable system for discovering, validating, prioritising, and operationalising high-probability adoption opportunities across diverse regional ecosystems. Broad geographic labels such as Africa, Latin America, or East Asia offer limited decision guidance. Adoption opportunities emerge not from geography alone, but from the intersection of stakeholder demand, institutional readiness, regulatory conditions, delivery capacity, infrastructure availability, and local ecosystem dynamics.

This research will produce evidence-based regional entry playbooks for Cardano — decision tools designed to enable the Product Committee and ecosystem stakeholders to distinguish actionable opportunities from cosmetic engagement, prioritise regions based on transparent criteria, and identify the conditions that must be satisfied before ecosystem resources are deployed.

Research Framing​

The research is framed around a single master question:

Which regional ecosystem configurations create the highest-probability pathway for sustained Cardano adoption, and what evidence thresholds must be satisfied before ecosystem resources are deployed?

This framing deliberately shifts from "which countries should Cardano enter" — a question that typically generates country lists rather than decisions — to "how does Cardano build a structured, evidence-based capability for regional ecosystem development." 3. Understanding of the Brief

3.1 The Decision Problem​

The RFP does not ask for a market survey. It asks for a decision architecture. The evidence gap the CPC identifies is not simply that Cardano lacks information about emerging markets — it is that Cardano stakeholders cannot currently distinguish between regional opportunities that warrant action and those that merely appear promising. Without that distinction, ecosystem resources risk flowing toward geographic narratives rather than viable entry plays.

The RFP is explicit about this. It warns against treating "LATAM", "Africa", or "East Asia" as coherent magnets, causing confusing event attendance or letters of intent with adoption signal, and against producing recommendations that cannot be connected to reachable counterparties, realistic regulatory pathways, and available delivery capacity. Every one of these failure modes reflects the same underlying problem: evidence that was descriptive rather than decision-grade.

This proposal is designed to produce decision-grade evidence.

3.2 The Eight Decision Gates​

The RFP defines eight explicit decision gates that the research must answer. Each gate corresponds to a specific question that Cardano stakeholders need to resolve before committing resources to a regional opportunity. The research design in this proposal is built around these gates, not around a pre-selected country list or a predetermined conclusion.

The eight gates, in summary, are:

  • Which regions should be prioritised for near-term government and emerging-market entry, based on comparative evidence rather than strategic narrative?
  • Which use cases are credible entry plays in each priority region, and which are aspirational without current evidence of viability?
  • Which counterparties are reachable within a 12–24 month horizon, and what route exists to decision-makers or implementation partners?
  • What does the regulatory landscape look like per priority region, and what dependencies or blockers affect the entry play?
  • What infrastructure prerequisites must be in place before action is credible, including stablecoin liquidity, off-ramp access, identity, privacy, mobile-first design, and delivery capacity?
  • What partner and delivery model is required in each region, and who can build, operate, support, and scale the solution?
  • What constitutes meaningful adoption in each play, and what should not be counted as evidence of progress?
  • Which findings belong in adjacent RFPs or workstreams, and what cross-portfolio handoffs are required to keep scope clean?

The methodology mapped in Section 6, the workplan in Section 14, and the deliverables in Section 15 are each mapped directly to these eight gates. No section of the research exists to answer one or more of them.

3.3 What This Proposal Is Not​

This proposal is not a government affairs strategy, a lobbying brief, or a diplomatic engagement plan. It is not a legal advisory document. It does not execute pilots, distribute grants, or represent Cardano in regulatory proceedings. These distinctions are stated explicitly in the RFP and are observed in full throughout this proposal.

The research will produce regulatory landscapes summaries that are decision-oriented and appropriately caveated. Where regulatory findings require specialist legal review before action, this proposal will say so clearly.

Similarly, this proposal does not attempt to fully answer RFP 01 or RFP 02. Customer segmentation and use-case landscape analysis are drawn upon to the extent that they are necessary to answer the RFP 05 decision gates. Findings that more properly belong in those RFPs will be captured in the Cross-RFP Handoff Memo rather than absorbed into an overextended scope here.

6. Research Design and Methodology

6.1 Research Design Philosophy​

The research follows a structured mixed-design method. It begins with a desk-research phase to establish hypotheses and a screening baseline, proceeds through primary evidence collection to test and revise those hypotheses, and concludes with a synthesis phase that converts tested evidence into decision-ready frameworks and playbooks.

The design is explicitly hypothesis-testing rather than hypothesis-confirming. The research will investigate candidate region opportunities without assuming in advance that any particular region is the correct answer. Regions, sub-regions, or country clusters that fail to demonstrate evidence-based viability will be recommended for deferral, monitoring, or no-go, not stretched to meet a predetermined conclusion.

The methodology is structured across seven workstreams. These workstreams operate in parallel during the primary research phase rather than sequentially, to ensure that regulatory findings, infrastructure assessments, and counterparty mapping can inform each other in real time.

6.6 Use-Case Assessment Framework​

Use-case assessment for each priority region will evaluate:

  • Problem Severity: Is the problem the use case addresses genuinely acute in this region? Is there evidence of unmet demand from local actors?
  • Cardano Fit: Does the specific use case require properties that Cardano provides — deterministic settlement, private ledger, identity primitives, privacy features — or would a centralised or conventional solution serve equally well?
  • Competitive Alternatives: What alternatives currently exist — both blockchain and non-blockchain — and why or why not Cardano be preferred?
  • Stakeholder Demand: Have local actors — not Cardano advocates — expressed specific demand for the kind of solution this use case represents?
  • Dependencies: Does the use case require stablecoin liquidity, off-ramp access, identity infrastructure, delivery partners, or regulatory approval before it is actionable?
  • Adoption Pathway: Is there a concrete pathway from initial pilot to recurring usage, and who owns each step of that pathway?

6.7 Regulatory Mapping Methodology​

Regulatory mapping will be conducted at a decision-oriented, landscape level appropriate for informing go/no-go, further diligence, and local counsel recommendations. It is not intended to replace formal legal advice, and this distinction will be clearly stated in all deliverables.

For each priority region, the regulatory mapping will identify:

  • Relevant regulatory bodies and their posture toward blockchain, digital assets, payments, and data
  • Applicable licensing, permission, or registration requirements for candidate use cases
  • Stablecoin, cryptocurrency, and payment regulatory status
  • Data protection, privacy, and public-chain transparency considerations
  • Public procurement rules and pathways
  • Foreign exchange and capital control implications
  • Uncertainty levels and recommended further diligence actions

6.9 Bias Controls​

The methodology incorporates explicit bias controls at the research design, recruitment, data collection, and analysis stages:

  • Cardano Insider Optimism: Primary research will actively seek non-Cardano stakeholders, including those who evaluated Cardano and chose alternatives, as validation counterweights.
  • Regional Overgeneralisation: The RARF framework operates at country-cluster level, not continental level, to prevent broad geographic labels from masking intra-regional variation.
  • English-Language Bias: PRISMA's hub network in sub-Saharan Africa includes access to participants in non-English operating environments. Where local-language primary research is required, this will be documented and the limitation noted where full coverage is not possible.
  • Elite and Capital-City Bias: The ALJ methodology deliberately surfaces grassroots and implementation-level actors who are typically absent from capital-city-focused research. This is one of the distinctive advantages of PRISMA.
  • Access Overclaiming: All counterparty access will be classified as Interviewed, Warm Access, Referred, Desk-Identified, or Hypothetical. Findings based on Hypothetical or Desk-Identified sources will be clearly marked with lower confidence levels.
  • Treating Intent as Adoption: The CASS framework is the operational control for this risk. No signal will be classified above its evidence-supported tier.

13. Adoption Signal Plan

13.1 CASS Application Protocol​

The Cardano Adoption Signal Standard (CASS) introduced in Section 6.4 will be applied consistently across all evidence collected during the research. The following protocol governs how CASS classifications are assigned:

  • Every adoption signal identified during primary or desk research will be assigned a CASS tier at the time it is recorded
  • Signals that are currently cited in Cardano community discussion will be reviewed and reclassified where the evidence basis does not support the tier implied by community reporting

  • Tier 1 classification requires multiple corroborating primary sources and documentary evidence of recurring usage
  • All CASS classifications will be included in the evidence register with their source, date, and confidence level

13.2 Cosmetic Signal Identification​

The research will explicitly identify signals that have been cited as adoption evidence but do not meet CASS Tier 2 or above standards. Where the research finds that a prior community claim about regional progress represents Tier 4 (Weak Signal) evidence, this will be reported clearly, with the basis for the reclassification explained.

This is not a negative assessment of Cardano's regional activities — it is a necessary function of responsible research. Resources should not be deployed based on inflated signal assessments.

13.3 Signal-to-Action Translation​

The Research-to-Action Roadmap will translate CASS classifications into action recommendations:

  • Tier 1 (Verified Adoption): Document and publish as evidence; identify what would scale the opportunity further
  • Tier 2 (Strong Signal): Recommend go or conditional-go; specify conditions and owner
  • Tier 3 (Moderate Signal): Recommend conditional-go with specific dependency resolution required; specify next evidence milestone
  • Tier 4 (Weak Signal): Recommend monitoring; specify what evidence upgrade would justify further investment

20. Confidentiality and Data Handling Approach

20.1 Consent and Attribution​

All primary research participants will be informed in advance of the purpose of the research, who commissioned it, how their responses will be used, and the expected output formats. Each participant will be asked to confirm their preferred attribution status before any interaction is recorded: attributable, anonymised, or fully confidential.

No recording will be made without explicit prior consent. Where consent is given for recording, recordings will be stored in encrypted format and access will be limited to the core research team.

20.2 Data Classification​

Research outputs will be classified into four data categories:

  • Public Findings: Suitable for community publication without modification. Included in the public summary.
  • CPC-Only Findings: Suitable for the Product Committee and Interest governance purposes, but not for public publication. Includes commercially sensitive counterparty information, sensitive government views, and preliminary findings that require further validation before public release.
  • Confidential Raw Data: Interview notes, transcripts, and raw evidence. Not to be published in any form. Retained securely by PRISMA for the duration of the project and deleted according to the agreed retention schedule.
  • Restricted / Proprietary Data: Paid or third-party datasets used to inform findings. CPC can inspect methodology and findings derived from these sources, but the underlying data cannot be published unless separately licensed.

20.3 Storage and Access Controls​

All raw data will be stored in encrypted, access-controlled cloud storage with access limited to named members of the research team. Recordings and transcripts will be deleted within 12 months of project completion, or immediately following final report delivery where respondents request earlier deletion.

20.4 Publication Boundaries​

The public summary will include methodology overview, key findings, publishable recommendations, and evidence caveats. It will not expose: respondent identities (unless explicit public attribution consent was obtained); sensitive government or regulatory views; commercially sensitive or implementation details; security-sensitive information; or findings that would put respondents at institutional, regulatory, and political risk.

21. Ethics Approach

This research involves primary interactions with human participants, including public-sector officials, practitioners in sensitive institutional contexts, and community members in developing-market environments. The following minimum safeguards will apply throughout:

  • All participants will be informed of the research purpose, commissioning organisation, intended outputs, and distribution channels before any interaction
  • Participation is voluntary; participants may withdraw consent at any time without affecting their relationship with PRISMA or the Cardano ecosystem
  • Where participants are in institutional contexts where expressing views on blockchain or digital assets could create professional or regulatory risk, PRISMA will explicitly offer fully anonymous participation and will not push for attribution against participant preferences
  • PRISMA will not mislead participants about the purpose or audience of the research, the likely use of their contributions, or the confidentiality treatment of their responses
  • Extra care will be applied in public-sector, humanitarian, and community contexts where institutional or political risk is more acute
  • Where community or beneficiary-level participants are included in any component of the research, PRISMA will ensure that their participation does not create economic, social, or safety risks
  • No personally identifiable information beyond what is required for research contact management will be collected or stored

PRISMA will prepare a research ethics brief before fieldwork begins, aligned to broadly accepted principles of responsible research practice. This brief will be shared with the CPC at the Research Design Review checkpoint.

22.4 Budget Defence​

The budget is structured to maximise evidence value per ADA spent. The largest single line — ADA 16,000 for primary research — reflects the non-negotiable reality that desk research alone cannot

answer the counterparty accessibility, delivery capacity, and genuine adoption signal questions that the RFP demands. Without this investment, the research would produce a descriptive market report, not a decision architecture.

The regional network access line — ADA 8,500 — reflects real costs that a conventional research firm would face at a much higher level, given that they would need to build regional access from scratch. PRISMA's existing hub relationships reduce this substantially; this line covers the coordination, facilitation, and logistics of activating an existing network, not building a new one.

The contingency line — ADA 5,000 — is explicitly for two scenarios: first, if access constraints require alternative recruitment strategies (adding respondent compensates for inaccessible institutional actors); and second, if preliminary findings identify a regional opportunity with unusually high strategic value that warrants additional primary research for improved confidence. It will not be deployed without CPC visibility and agreement.
